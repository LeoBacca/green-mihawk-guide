## Vs Enel (Purple Enel)

**Die roll:** First

**Game Plan:** Full Value / Control by default — but constantly look for opportunities to change gears

This is probably one of the least autopilot matchups in the guide.

### Mulligan Heuristics

Mulligan for 2+ searchers or 1 searcher + 5c

Going 2nd hand is very solid ,  the 2 mihawk spells can also help either keep our life count up when he swings for 7s or contest his early pieces like franky/law/ reiju/ pink, or even out an early ohm.

Going 1st hand is unplayable if he starts ohm ohm , so i'd mulligan.

### Early Game

Start by reading Enel's opening.

You can play value/control.

You can starve initially, control their board, establish 1–2 10c Shanks, and only then transition into a two-turn clock.

Or, if your hand supports it, you can race.

Having Electrical Luna already available makes aggression much more attractive because you know you may eventually create one or two extremely powerful Electrical Luna turns.

But their development matters just as much as your hand.

#### If they open holly

Racing becomes less attractive.

Holly gives them efficient early pressure, so slowing the game down and controlling their board becomes more appealing.

#### If they open ohm->ohm

Now we have almost the opposite conclusion.

We're realistically not winning a traditional value war.

Multiple Ohm are simply too efficient at clearing our board while developing theirs.

So stop trying to beat them at the game their opening is designed to win.

Start looking for aggression.

### Mid Game

Continuously reassess.

Maybe you started by starving.

Then Enel misses development and your board gets wide.

Change gears.

Maybe you started aggressively because you had Electrical Luna, but their development makes the race increasingly bad.

Change gears.

Maybe Ohm → Ohm tells you your board isn't going to survive a prolonged value game.

Accept that your characters are eventually going to die and ask how much damage they can generate before they die.

### Late Game

Our control line wants to establish 1–2 10c Shanks and then compress the damage phase into a short clock.

Our aggressive line wants to leverage Electrical Luna to deny the opponent use of the board they've developed while we continue pushing face.

The late game therefore depends heavily on the route we took to reach it.

### Key Matchup Concepts

Purple Enel is almost a pure test of Mihawk's macro philosophy.

Don't enter the matchup saying:

"I'm starving."

or:

"I'm racing."

Instead ask:

"What is Enel giving me, and which game plan punishes it hardest?"

Holly can push you toward control.

Ohm → Ohm can push you toward aggression.

Your own Electrical Luna can make a race much more attractive.

The correct plan emerges from the information you're given.

### Matchup Summary

**Default → Value / Control**

Then:

Read opening → Evaluate clocks and resources → Choose gear → Reassess continuously → Change gears when necessary

The mistake is not choosing control or aggro.

**The mistake is continuing to play the same game after the information in front of you tells you that game is losing.**

### Reading their opening: Holly vs Ohm

A very important part of this matchup is understanding what Enel's early development tells us about the kind of game we should play.

For example, an early Holly can make racing significantly less efficient.

Their clock is already developing quickly, so we may prefer to slow the game down, control their board, and move toward our 10c Shanks turns before transitioning into aggression.

Ohm can create the exact opposite situation.

If our opponent starts something like:

Ohm → Ohm

we should immediately recognize that we're probably not winning a traditional value war.

Those copies of Ohm are simply too efficient at clearing our board while simultaneously developing theirs.

If we keep trying to play a slow game where both players trade resources and fight for board advantage, we're playing directly into what their opening is best at doing.

And this is exactly where the ability to change gears becomes important.

If Enel's opening tells you that you're going to lose the value war, stop trying to win the value war.

An opening like Ohm → Ohm should make us seriously consider accelerating the game and putting much more pressure on their Leader.

We accept that our board may eventually get cleared.

Instead of asking how we can protect every character and continue extracting value from them, we start asking how much damage those characters can generate before they disappear.

This becomes even more attractive if we already have access to Electrical Luna.

We can pressure aggressively, allow Enel to continue developing their board, and potentially use Electrical Luna later to create the extra turn we need to finish the game.

That's the contrast I want you to understand:

**Early Holly → Control becomes more attractive.**

**Early Ohm → Aggro becomes more attractive.**

Not because these are rigid rules, but because each opening changes the relative efficiency of our available game plans.

The matchup is constantly giving us information.

Our job is to react to it before we find ourselves five turns deep into a game plan that was already losing three turns ago.
