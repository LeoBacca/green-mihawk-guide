## Big Picture

> Going into Nationals / the Flame Flame Fruit weekend there are **three or four decks you can really consider**: **Mihawk, Robin, Enel and RG Luffy-Ace**; "everything else is kind of mid". Mihawk and Robin are "that little bit better than everything else", Enel and Luffy-Ace are right there. Rocks beats everything that beats Enel and Robin, but loses to Mihawk. Pick the one you are most comfortable with, not the one the meta tells you to FOMO into.
>
> The one real change of the week is the **rest spell** — I Never Bother to Remember the Faces of Trash (OP14-038, the "trash event") — which "became more popular because it did very well and got a lot of good publicity over the last week". A free tap-and-trade tool paid by the leader that turns the early-game decks that used to prey on Mihawk (Sabo, Enel) into good matchups and makes the deck a **board-control deck first**, aggro second. "Sabo has gotten a lot worse this week" because of it. Mihawk overperformed last weekend: 5 of the top 8 at the big regional, 11 in the top cut vs 6 Robin and 5 Enel.

## 1. The Rest Spell: Mihawk Becomes a Board-Control Deck

- **What it does:** lets you tap your searchers / 1-drops and **trade into their things**; a keep-away game against Sabo and Enel, the two matchups that were tougher.
   - Before, you had to wait for **Yasopp** to deal with a T2 body; now you answer it on **turn 2**.
   - It is a **spell that costs nothing** because the leader pays for it: "spells cost nothing because your leader lets you do it for free".
- **The exchange math** on the early trade: you used one card from hand that replaced itself → **zero cards spent**; they either lose the character (lose a card) or counter and **lose two cards** to protect it.
- **Keep removing:** spell + Yasopp + Law + Yasopp again, every turn, until you have won the board. You are **full-starving Enel and Sabo** until the board is won.
   - Once the board is won: one turn to pull cards out of their hand, one turn to take a lot of life, next turn kill — or life first, then cards, then kill.
- **Rule:** removal without a board is just removal — "if you're not ahead on board, your opponent just plays another thing". You need the prerequisite attackers in play; the **best use of the spell is their early 1-drops**.
   - Removing their early 1s also **denies them a Law** on the 6-DON turn. Minimum 3 Vander Decken in every build.
- **Contest a searcher-light hand:** if they only have two searchers, Yasopp one and rest-spell the other → deny the 6-5 on the 6-DON turn. "The most interesting thing you can do with it."
- **Late game** the card is even more impactful: with 4 stages you counter out of a 10k Shanks swing with **Event + DON**, or **Event, Event**. "Super chill if you can get into those positions."
- **Most of the time the gameplay is still standard.** "It's not really changing too much — don't completely mess your head up about it": the card is a flexible tool that lets you do additional things on top of the normal Mihawk game.
- **It complements Yasopp, it does not replace him.** "More of the same effect to do more of the same thing throughout the game." Never cut Yasopp: draws a card, kills their guy, re-stands itself — "what card is this?".
   - Even in the hybrid Hawk build Impact kept 3 Yasopp. More Hawks is not the way.
- **Coffin Boat is the best card in the deck** and the second Boat is literally free with the rest spell: use the leader DON on the spell, tap the old boat, play the new boat = a 0-cost draw.
- **Commit to 4 copies.** It is not a tech card, it is a whole game plan: rest → remove → repeat until the board lead is big enough to "blast them 7-7-7-7-7 and melt them".
   - Is it a sacrifice in the mirror? No: "you're not making sacrifices to play it" — the list is good into the mirror too.
- **Why the rest build over the Perona/Yasopp tempo build:** rest is good into all the random decks (Enel, Sabo, Robin, Rocks, Luffy-Ace, Kaido); Peronas are better in the mirror. "You never play against the same deck five times in a tournament" → cover the random stuff. "Fighting Enel is miserable if you don't have it, especially if you lose the roll."
- **Edge in the mirror with the rest build:** rest their 1-drops so they cannot Law on the 6-DON turn, then you 6-5 them on 7 DON and you are up three. Same idea as Stun-Bonney on a DON, except you also deny next turn. "Everything that rests once also draws a card."
- **Keep with the rest spell vs aggro:** rest spell + stage + unit is a keep. Searchers are still the priority.

[Flow: THE REST-SPELL LOOP (VS SABO / ENEL / EARLY-GAME DECKS)]

1. **T1: stage + any 1-drop** — Kikunojo, Tama, Perona, Kin'emon…
2. **T2: tap DON + stage + 1-drop** — rest spell: hit their 2-drop for 8, draw a card
3. **They lose the body or spend 2 cards** — you spent 0 cards from hand
4. **T3+: Yasopp / Law / spell again** — full starve: no life cards for them
5. **Board won** — they have no bricks to counter with
6. **Cards → life → kill** — 2-3 turns, 6k leader + sized bodies

*Rule: the spell only works when you already have attackers on board. Best target: their early 1-drops.*

### 2.2  Card-by-card verdicts

- **Stronger > Luna when you play 4 stages.** One spell for free every turn; even used as a 2k "who cares" — the opponent never gets multiple cards off a 7k swing. Cut the Lunas for Strongers; keep some freezes (2 max) for Luffy-Ace.
   - Why Elijah cut Luna: in the mirror you board-control them anyway, so you don't need it.
   - Vs Luffy-Ace Luna is objectively better, but Law + Oden + stun spell gets the same effect: "you just need Luna to freeze enough that you survive enough that you can kill them".
   - Vs Sabo Luna is useless (their guys are +12 cost); Stronger at worst is a counter and still stops the same swings.
   - **Two freezes are enough:** "you only need to see one per game in this build" — the opponent has fewer things in play to freeze. They are mostly for Luffy-Ace and the odd Robin game where you go "I'm racing you now: Shanks and freeze".
   - No Luna scary in the mirror? "Nah." With the rest spell you board-control one more unit, so Stronger does the same job; once you control enough, Stronger is still a defensive spell while Luna does nothing.
- **4 Shanks > 3 Shanks + 1 Bepo.** "Seeing Shanks-Shanks is better than seeing Shanks-Bepo in almost any matchup." Bepo = a defensive 10-drop that buys one more turn (pseudo-blank all their 6k bodies + stun the big body ≈ Decken + Luna in one turn), for the **second-to-last turn** so the Shanks 11-12 lethal is more reliable.
   - When Law & Bepo is right: you need to board-control them the entire way and **stretch your Lunas one more turn** — become an 8k leader for a turn, keep the Luna in hand, and still push a damage attacking for 10-11 with the freed DON.
   - "I'm still not convinced it's better than the 4th Shanks. It certainly can be — that scenario comes up fairly often."
- **4 Otama, never Kikunojo instead.** "Kikunojo is an Otama that always whiffed; Otama is a Kikunojo that sometimes draws a card." The re-stand is fake: a 1-drop in play gets rest-spelled / Yasopped anyway. Only add Kiku as extra Otama targets, never cut Otama for it.
   - The real cost: Otama whiffs "maybe one game in 20" more than before; Kikunojo "whiffs 100% of the time". You will feel dumb playing Kiku on the 7-DON turn to enable Law when an Otama would have done the same and sometimes drawn.
- **Samurai:** 2 is fine; you cannot cut it completely ("it needs to hit some of the time"). With the rest spell your 1-drops stick more because you tap theirs → Samurai resolves **more** often.
- **DMG costs 1 in this deck** — "the best DMG deck of all time" (after GP Luffy). Tap the Robin DON on the last turn and still attack with 9 DON; tap the Mom then Decken her early; push through a DON + a Blocker for lethal in the mirror.
   - **How to fit it:** cut a Beam for the DMG ("not the worst thing in the world"), or go 1 Stronger / 1 DMG.
- **5-cost Perona:** multiple copies is valid (the winner did). In the aggro build the opponent cannot Yasopp-bomb her; good into rest-spell decks, Enel and Luffy-Ace ("Perona owns Luffy-Ace").
- **Stun Bonney:** not greedy, just another tech — stun their DON on the 9-DON turn and keep them off Shanks for a turn. Makes going first less "cancer" in the mirror.
- **Paradise Waterfall** is the upgrade Impact would play in Mihawk: when they Shanks you, Waterfall **picks up the Law** (out of Luna) and you block the Shanks swing with it. Also very good in Luffy-Ace: when they Luna you, Waterfall is a blowout.
- **Hody Jones:** tested in the VOD. Some games the best card in the pool, some games unplayable because you cannot take a life; at worst it is still a fish.
- **Two Decken?** No.
- **Hybrid split:** if you go hybrid, 3 Law 2 Hawk instead of 4-1 — "to lessen my reliance on 1-drops: if people are playing rest spells I want to make sure I can play my Mihawks".

## 3. Mihawk Mirror: Counter Discipline and Sequencing

- **Life plan:** take the first two life, defend the third. If you lost the board fight you may need to counter the fourth too, because you need time to fight back for the board.
- **Aggro version (Hawks + Peronas):** take the first two for free — Perona stuns two 5s and is hard to remove, Mihawk swings 8 with a DON, you win fast with Shanks.
   - In the aggro build you "don't care about your 1s": Samurai them away (you don't care about Law) and play max Samurai.
- **Sequencing going first:** Oden first so they Yasopp your Oden and then you Yasopp their Yasopp. Or use your Yasopp on their 1-drops so they cannot 6-5 you. Either way: keep your board-control tools to deal with their stuff.
- **The 1-DON tap line:** with little stuff in play, use the leader's free DON to tap down nonsense characters (a Perona, your stage), rest their guy and **bomb it for 8** — one avenue, not the only one.
- **When to defend a body:** "you defend if you think the body is going to help you board-control them back next turn; if not, let it die".
- **Search etiquette with the spell build:** take a Kin'emon over a Law if you are stranded on one 1-drop — "I'm so scared my opponent kills my one 1-drop and I can't play Law". Play the curve and aim for double 6-5s.

[Tree: 7 DON, THEY SWING 9 INTO YOUR 6K BODY: COUNTER?]

**Opponent swings 9k into your 6k body on 7 DON**

- **Your event is active → fine to counter** — one card, no problem
- **Body helps you board-control their character next turn → keep it** — counter if it costs 1 card
- **Aggressive version and the body swings 8 at life next turn → keep it** — "very chill as well"
- **It would cost 2-2 → less likely, let it die** — sequence so you can still attack their things back

## 4. Playing From Behind: Find the Win Condition in Your Hand

- **First look at your cards and decide what your win condition is.** "My hand is just all those cards, that is the game I'm trying to play."
- **You are not just playing the cards in your hand but the cards in your deck.** If the plan is play Shanks, swing out, top-deck another Shanks, top-deck another Shanks — "that's just how it is".
- **Envision the world where they miss.** "If they had the pieces, you weren't winning anyway." Take more risks in a losing position: your range beats fewer cards, so play around only what you can actually beat.
   - "If you can't play around anything because you don't beat anything, then your opponent has to have nothing. That's just the play you have to make."

[Tree: WHICH GAME AM I PLAYING? (HAND-DEPENDENT)]

**Missed development / read your hand**

- **A bunch of Shanks** — all damage face, race, not a resource battle
- **5s that replace themselves** — board-control game: Yasopp + bomb one guy, lessen their double summons, slow the game
- **No searchers, they have searchers** — only one win con: push, be aggressive

## 5. The Other Decks: What Impact Would Play and Build

### 5.1  Robin

- **Still Robin, still very good** — "the literal first day I played OP17 I played Robin and the deck was OP". Its worst matchup is maybe the 7th most popular deck; you don't want a deck with a personal auto-loss to the 1st or 2nd most popular deck.
- **Yost's upgrade: 4 Borsalino** — "this is the nuts". Fewer triggers is not a concern: you heal more life so you get more trigger chances. Gives a consistent mid-game turn going second (take, take, Borsa swing 7, heal, go to 10 DON); later Borsa + Yamato + Giant is the nuts against everyone except Mihawk, and even there you heal twice.
- **Vs RG Luffy-Ace (the only real bad matchup):** dodge the turn-2 combo, ramp, heal, and at 1 life take all your swings, play Big Mom, burn them. Ramp → banish, Borsalino swing 7 to get them to 2 and take 2 banishes.
- **Vs Mario Bros:** the sketchy line — jam them T2, banish, Yamato, banish again, hit triggers, Mom burn. "Relies on your four random cards in life, but that is better than 0%."
- **Vs Rocks:** take **second**: Teach on turn 3, Big Mom on turn 4, fast ramp with big threats. "A lot more chill than you think."
- **Mirror:** count the leader swings; use Ovens to poke 5s — not much difference between a 5 and an 8 when both hands are low.

### 5.2  Enel

- **Build:** lots of 1s, Laws, Frankys, Pinks, Enels. **4 Enel** (Kaido made top 4 with 3 and they agreed on 4): vs Robin you need Enel-Enel-Enel because they heal and you need "a comically large swing to push past the heal"; vs other decks you need big Enels standing.
   - 2 Divine Departure for the bricks; **2-2 split Gamma Knife / Gum-Gum Lightning** (not 3-1) so Pink can toolbox either.
   - **No dog:** "a pretty stuff card if everyone's playing the rest spell" — the dog just dies.
   - Biggest edge in the mirror: more Pinks; Enel is chill too.
- **Sequence removal:** rested body → Gamma Knife; active bodies → Gum-Gum Lightning. Use non-Pink tools first so Pink fetches exactly the tool you need.
- **Develop 1s:** on a 4-drop turn go 4-1-1-1; on an Enel turn you don't have to swing 6-6 — play Enel, swing 5-6, play a 1. Always have 1s in play for the leader skill.
- **Vs Boa:** "cooked" — they keep healing. Max big Enels, Pink to spam Lightning Dragon and Mama on every 5 (each is a +1), Enel-Enel-Enel to have enough attacks. A student lost 10 of 10 test games with a 2-Enel list; "with just two you can win pretty decently" but you need the 4th.
- **Vs Mono Teach:** pseudo-starve; sandbag the 1s until you can play a bunch in one turn; as many big Enels standing as possible; chip with the leader.
   - **Don't donate your 1s** — you want the life cards. Once you have a board of 1s, put DON on them and swing 5 (one of them swings for free) while you pseudo-starve and build up.

### 5.3  RG Luffy-Ace

- **On the up-trend** — one RG Luffy in the top 8 of the small Athens regional (3 Enel, 3 Mihawk, 1 RG Luffy, 1 Kaido). Recommended for fast-paced online matchmaking: "your games last 5 seconds".
- **Vs Mihawk:** combo on the 5-DON turn, then another Rush; take them to 1; then defend with events for back-to-back turns and use Strongers so you only counter the leader swing. Double Luna = you're dead, one Luna you can tank a turn.
   - **2k-check early, then switch to 7s.** "Damage, damage, damage." Conqueror's Haki gets you out of all the 6s; they swing 7 to play around it; the Whitebeard spell plays around 7s.
   - **Leave the correct amount of DON** for their attacks — "don't hurt yourself for free"; one extra DON vs a wide board to play around DMG.
   - Sabo the event is very strong (searches, trigger searches) and you get Radical Beam, the only real "radical beam".
- **Which combo piece to search:** grab the less searchable one; here they are all Straw Hats so take the one you've already seen and passed in a previous search; otherwise dealer's choice.

### 5.4  Rocks, Sabo, Boa, Black Luffy, Nami

- **Rocks:** good into everything except Mihawk — "it beats everything that beats Enel, it beats Robin, and it beats all the things that beat Robin"; Gloriosa FTKs Luffy-Ace. Play it only if you think you can dodge Mihawk; it punishes Mihawks that go "oh, easy win", never tested the matchup and let the board snowball into Captain John + Usopp spell.
   - Vs Mihawk as Rocks: pressure life while developing, attack 6 and play the odd curve with the leader, Newgate then Newgate skill to attack 8; Kyo on 2, Wang on 4, Newgate on 6, Shiki; Captain John + Usopp spell to force them into the John while you pitch cards to keep it alive.
   - Rocks' own plan into Mihawk: pressure life while defending your board; when they Luna you ("an inevitability") play Captain John + Usopp spell.
- **Sabo:** worse if people play the rest spell; "if you register Sabo this week, you're going to have a bad time" (vs Mihawk with practice). Vs Robin: outlast, spin the wheels, play hand-neutral waves (Usopp, Docking, Luffy), attack with multiple units not one at a time, swing 7s not 6s ("she plays a ton of 2k"), Loki deals with Pudding while adding an attacker.
- **Black Luffy with Ice Age:** decent, not fully tested; Great Eruption + Ice Age + Loki kills their 6-5 on the 7-DON turn; Paradise Waterfall in Luffy-Ace blows out a Luna.
   - What is strong: the **6-5 on the 7-DON turn** and Ice Age on a Franky mid-game — "nothing super deep"; Impact would still just play Mihawk.
- **Boa:** not that bad, "attack 5 a bunch of times"; beats Enel but loses miserably to Robin → scratch it. Gets abused by players who know not to attack early.
- **Nami:** no — Mihawk, Luffy-Ace, Kaido-style ramp and Sabo all fill the board with 6s and swing 7s. "Where are the matchups where Nami would be good? I just don't see it."
- **Which deck to register:** "whichever one you pick, pick the one you are the most comfortable with" — in a long tournament comfort with your deck and matchups matters more than the last-minute edge. If Mihawk, play the rest spell: do the early board control, then Mihawk shines going wide with Laws and spinning the wheels.

## 6. Tournament Weekend: Body, Head and Field

- **Sleep:** go to bed an hour earlier than you think you should, "because it takes about an hour for your mind to really calm down". Stressors or worries before the tournament → **write them on a piece of paper**: the brain relaxes, you can come back to them later, "you will have a better sleep".
   - **Caffeine** (if you are a caffeine person) at strategic timings: not first thing when you wake up — wait until you've been awake about an hour, then a second caffeine break around the halfway point of the day.
   - Friday: dinner and scrims after, but "don't burn out too much on a Friday before the Flame Flame on the Saturday" — don't stay up too late.
- **Snacks and water:** small snacks all day for consistent energy; carry the water bottle — "if you are dehydrated it is as bad for you as not sleeping well".
- **It is mostly a learning experience.** If you lose in a frustrating way, write down how you lost and the spot, say you will review it later — "that is enough for your brain to allow you to move on". Don't tilt.
- **Marathon, not a sprint:** play at an appropriate pace so you finish your games, but don't make "stupid mistakes because you're rushing too quickly". Think about how the game looks when you win it and **work your way backwards**: take the searches and cards that move toward that endgame.
   - A lot of it is hand-dependent, but a lot is matchup understanding: "if you're listening to how I've described these matchups you can tell which cards you actually need — I'm basically telling you what you need to do".
- **On stream / people hovering:** "the only thing I care about when I'm playing a match is playing the match to the best of my ability". Impact's edge: 10 years of esports and "a million times" on stream — you hear white noise plus your opponent through the mic and you block it out.
- **The Nats field:** people take it more seriously than a regional, but no LCQ and many store-champ qualifiers → player quality is still around the middle. The difference: no "round-1 Jimmy" decks — you play the good decks: lots of Mihawk, Robin, Enel, some Luffy-Ace and Sabo.
- **Same deck for every event.** Don't FOMO into a different deck for Nats vs Flame Flame vs the next one; play the deck you are comfortable with the entire way through.
- **After winning:** don't tell them "I just got lucky" — say "you played really well, I think you'll win" and keep their OWR high.

## Matchups

### vs Rocks (as Mihawk)

**Verdict:** favored ("free" only if you board control) — Luna makes it unlosable **Turn order:** second preferred, first is still fine

- **Assessment:** you are a **6k leader**, so they must commit DON to swing you with the leader and play **off-curve**. Once the board is won it takes little effort: 6k leader + decently sized bodies, and with no characters in play they cannot use random bricks in hand as counter — "they just die quickly".
- **Plan — board control, not starve:** Shanks, Vander Decken and Yasopp are removal tools first. Decken their 10-12; Yasopp + Decken kills Shiki. Swings go to life only when convenient, after their board is dealt with; then repeat.
   - They must discard a card for the leader to attack 8 and defend their board constantly; with no life cards from you they run out. Rest everything, Shanks, Decken, clear the team, repeat.
- **Trap — the all-face route:** go all face then Luna their board, and Captain John + Usopp spell "Lunas you back": you cannot deal with their board productively and cannot kill them, turns trade back and forth, and "if they have more Usopp spells and cards than you have attacks, you're in trouble".
- **Captain John rule:** when they go John + Usopp spell, get as many cards as you can out of your hand that turn and **Oden the John** so it cannot press again — no back-to-back John + Usopp with the same John. Then take them out.

[Columns: TWO ROUTES VS ROCKS]

#### All face, then Luna the board

- They answer with **Captain John + Usopp spell** = they "Luna you back"
- You can't clear the board productively and can't kill them
- Turns trade back and forth: more Usopp spells than your attacks → you lose

#### Board control first (Impact)

- You are a 6k leader: they go off-curve to swing you; no board = bricks in hand

### vs Robin (as Mihawk)

**Verdict:** slightly unfavored, ~45-55 ("Robin is still Robin")

- **Assessment:** "Robin is still Robin", slightly ahead for her, ~55-45, but it is a gradual board-control game, not a race: remove a couple of early characters so that **Big Mom is just a 10-mana heal + draw**, not two 8k rushers into your life the turn she lands. Develop while picking apart their board — the spell lets you develop and spend only the free DON.
- **Early game — Yasopp is really important:** he taps but stands himself back up, so you never get Sweet Generals'd. "I try not to turn my bodies" when Robin has a board she can trade into; if she doesn't, turning them is fine.
   - With Yasopps in play you can tank a Teach hit later, no problem; if your plan is to turn Laws and Odens, keep cards to protect them.
- **Counter discipline:** take the first two life against Robin most of the time. Take the second banish too unless you have a super redundant 1k (a third 5-drop you won't play): throw it away to tank a bigger swing later — only if you don't plan on turning your Laws. Robin is trying to run you out of resources.
- **Pressure with 5-5, not one big swing:** when their hand is low, refresh DON onto Perona / Kin'emon and attack 5-5. Early they give up so many cards to ramp, and triggered bodies don't refill the hand, so small swings force a counter or a double take — instead of one big swing "they happily take and then just heal".
- **Shanks as a board-control tool:** tap their things, swing under them, Decken the Mom, then attack her; or Shanks and **blast the Teach** — either they Giant and still lose 2-3 cards, or they lose him. "Their next turn is super slow": no cards to defend their stuff.
   - Use a couple of Shanks to run them out of cards, then out of relevant threats; your board sticks, they play heals and you slowly chip through the heals.
- **Law is the best card in the matchup:** picks up Decken for later, resets Peronas, attacks when they have nothing to attack into it, and eventually blocks a 12. "Spam your Laws."
- **Go wide** so even after Sweet Generals they can't put weenie attacks into your guys; a leader swing into a rested 3k is one Radical Beam. Switch from the 4k bodies to life once their board is under control.
- **DMG line:** tap down the Mom's DON and Decken the Mom early — a valid way to stop the snowball.
- **Banishes and Oven:** you "counter" the banishes by drawing extra cards elsewhere (stage, Yasopp) to make up for the missing life cards. Oven: ignore it — they rarely have the DON to put it down (maybe exactly the 7-DON turn); clear everything around it and freeze it with Oden / Stronger until it does nothing.

[Tree: ROBIN SWINGS THE BANISH LEADER AT YOU: TAKE OR COUNTER?]

**Banish hit incoming** — Robin is trying to run you out of resources

- **First two life → take (most of the time)** — you "counter" banishes by drawing elsewhere: stage, Yasopp
- **Redundant 1k in hand (third 5-drop you won't play) → throw it away** — tank a bigger swing later — only if you won't turn your Laws
- **Your plan is to turn Laws / Odens → keep cards, take the banish** — protect the bodies you will attack with
- **Your board is Yasopps → take freely** — they re-stand: no Sweet Generals, you can tank a Teach hit later
- **Their hand is low + board controlled → go life 5-5** — Perona / Kin'emon swings force counters or double takes; not one 8 they take and heal

*Big Mom must land as "a 10-mana heal and draw", not two 8k rushers. Shanks on the Teach: Giant + 2-3 cards lost, or he dies → their next turn is super slow.*

### vs Kaido (as Mihawk)

**Verdict:** super good **Turn order:** first almost always

- **Assessment:** "a super good matchup — it's hard for them to win". Take first almost always.
- **First developed characters = Yasopps**: they dodge the leader skill by re-standing; later they stand the Shanks back up so Kaido can't Big-Mom it easily.
- **Pressure the life** so they don't get "infinity turns". Scale attacks oddly if needed: swing 7 with characters so they must use leader skill + a card, even if the leader swings 5. Don't spread attacks so they save a free card with the skill.
- **Plan for their heal:** Kaido has to take early damage because it needs pieces. Get them to 3, they heal to 4, get them to 2; your guys stand back up while they develop. Mid game: Odens + stun the King while pressuring. Poke cards out, fish the leader skill on a 6, then Shanks + Decken one guy and blast the other.
- Easy win con: Shanks puts them at 10-12, next turn 10-12-12.

[Flow: MIHAWK VS KAIDO: TAKE FIRST AND PRESS]

1. **Go first** — deny the easy ramp curve, extra attacker for you
2. **Yasopps first** — they re-stand: the leader skill whiffs; later they re-stand Shanks
3. **Pressure life early** — 7s with characters so they need leader skill + a card; leader can swing 5
4. **They heal 3 → 4** — plan for one extra turn: Kaido needs pieces, takes early damage
5. **Odens + stun the King** — poke cards out, fish the leader skill on a 6
6. **Shanks + Decken one, blast the other** — they're at 10-12; next turn 10-12-12

*Don't spread attacks: they save a free card with the leader skill. "It's hard for them to win."*

### vs Enel (as Mihawk)

**Verdict:** went from bad to really good with the rest spell

- **Assessment:** from bad to really good with the rest spell — "fighting Enel is miserable if you don't have it, especially if you lose the roll". Full starve + board control.
- **Plan:** don't give life cards. Their removal (Gamma Knife, Gum-Gum Lightning) doesn't replace itself, so every answer costs a card: "they have to continually find removal plus character". Play Shanks and bomb their Enel: with no life cards they have nothing to defend with.
- **Why it works:** your things draw when removed, so you keep playing more; you go low on life while starving them, but they run out of removal first. Something sticks, then force all their life in one turn — they get 1-2 turns max to use those cards.
   - When something sticks, play two more, remove their guy: eventually 3 things stick, then Shanks, then 4 — you win. Each attack asks 2 cards from their hand and they have few 2ks (two Sanji, a Mama 1k). Pre-play units on turn 1 (unit + stage or two units) so you can bomb the thing.

[Flow: FULL STARVE VS ENEL: THEY RUN OUT OF REMOVAL FIRST]

1. **T1: unit + stage / two units** — pre-play so you can bomb later
2. **Rest spell / Yasopp / Perona / Oden on their board** — no life cards for them
3. **They remove your guy** — Gamma Knife / Lightning don't replace themselves: -1 card each time
4. **Your guys draw when removed** — keep playing more; you go low on life, it's fine
5. **1-2 things stick → Shanks bombs the Enel** — they have no cards to defend
6. **3-4 things stick** — force all their life in one turn; they get 1-2 turns with those cards

*"They have to continually find removal plus character — not the easiest ask in the world." Each attack asks 2 cards; they have few 2ks.*

### vs Sabo (as Mihawk)

**Verdict:** went from bad to really good with the rest spell

- **Assessment:** from bad to really good with the rest spell. "Starve, especially if you're playing the spell" — if you attack life you never control their board, because they keep cards to defend it.
- **Plan (their perfect curve, they win the roll):** they play a 2 → you spell it with the leader. They play two 2s → Yasopp one. You are still at 5 life. They play Saul + a 2, maybe swing 5 with the 1 → counter with a junk card (it's just a 5). Then **spell again on the Saul**.
- **Why starving works:** if you remove the Saul that turn, "their Luffy doesn't have Rush the next turn"; remove the first couple of units and they cannot create board pressure back. Their 2-for-5s don't replace themselves unless it's Usopp / Docking Station — with Sanji they go down a card every time. "Your rest events are super good at board-controlling their stuff."
   - Once the board advantage is built: one turn to get cards out of their hand, one turn to take a lot of life, next turn kill (or life, cards, kill).
- **When to switch to face:** when they go **double 6-cost**. That also means something on your board probably stuck — from there chain Odens into those guys. It only works against those builds.

[Flow: THE STARVE LINE VS SABO (THEY WIN THE ROLL, PERFECT CURVE)]

1. **They play a 2** — you: rest spell with the leader, kill it
2. **They play two 2s** — you: Yasopp one — still 5 life
3. **They play Saul + a 2** — swing 5 with the 1? counter with a junk card
4. **You: spell again on Saul** — their Luffy has no Rush next turn
5. **Board lead built** — cards out of hand → life → kill (2-3 turns)
6. **Trigger: they go double 6-cost** — switch to face, chain Odens into the big guys

*Remove the first couple of units and Sabo cannot create board pressure back; their 2-for-5s don't replace themselves (except Usopp / Docking Station).*

### vs RG Luffy-Ace (as Mihawk)

**Verdict:** "you win the game the last turn before you die" **Turn order:** second is good

- **Assessment:** "you win the game the last turn before you die". You **cannot starve a deck whose leader gives everything Rush** and you cannot deny them any swings — you also have to race: "blast them, blast them, blast them".
- **Plan — a mid-range mirror:** go life, stun their 6s, swing 7s all game to empty their hand. You double-develop, so with 4 units one of yours stuns one of theirs — **Oden is the most important 5**. Cycle Peronas with Law to find +4s for their rushes.
- Build a board: 5, then 6-5, 6-5, kill with Shanks + Luna. Events in hand are mostly for getting out of swings. **8-DON turn:** Decken a guy, then 6-5 where the 5 is Oden stunning another → two bodies handled, next turn Shanks + Luna.
- **Life management:** counter early 5s with random 1ks; be at an **odd life total** when they double attack — 3 → 1, not 4 → 2 — to play around the double strike.
- Stronger does the job of Luna here because you mostly freeze the two Luffys (a DON on a searcher kills Sanji); Luna is still "objectively better" vs RG Luffy-Ace — keep 2 freezes.

[Flow: MID-RANGE MIRROR VS LUFFY-ACE: STUN AND RACE]

1. **5-drop** — counter early 5s with junk 1ks
2. **6-5, 6-5** — Oden stuns one of theirs each turn; cycle Peronas with Law for +4s
3. **8-DON turn** — Decken a guy, then 6-5 with Oden stunning another
4. **Sit at an odd life** — 3 → 1, not 4 → 2: play around the double strike
5. **Shanks + Luna / Stronger** — freeze the two Luffys; a DON on a searcher kills Sanji
6. **Win the last turn before you die** — you cannot starve Rush; blast them

*Events in hand are for getting out of swings. Oden is the most important 5.*

### vs Boa (as Mihawk)

- **Plan:** remove the non-Kuja stuff (Zoros, Namis) while building a board; aim to **double clear** two Kuja Pirates in one turn so you only take one. "Not really attack them until you build up a board, then attack all at once": Shanks, rest all their triggers, Luna.
- A couple of 7s just gets "take, take, trigger, trigger, heal": go wide enough to challenge them, counter out of their smaller swings, poke-poke-poke, then Shanks + Luna. Laws chump the big attacks later. Stun their guys: they turn two, you kill both and take one.

## Q&A Highlights

**Q** Register Sabo with practice or Mihawk with 16.5 experience?

**A** Mihawk. The mirror is pure fundamentals and you have them; "if you register Sabo this week you're going to have a bad time".

**Q** Rest build vs Perona/Yasopp build?

**A** Rest is good into all the random decks (Enel, Sabo, Robin); Peronas are better in the mirror. "You never play against the same deck five times" → Impact takes the rest spell.

**A** Still Robin. More Luffy-Ace this week, but Robin's worst matchup is maybe the 7th most popular deck.

**A** No — the event complements Yasopp, more of the same effect all game long.

## Quick Reference Card

1. Four decks only: Mihawk, Robin, Enel, RG Luffy-Ace. Play the one you are most comfortable with for every event of the weekend; Rocks only if you can dodge Mihawk.
2. Mihawk = board control first. 4 Faces of Trash (the rest spell), 3-4 Coffin Boat, Stronger over Luna (≤2 freezes for Luffy-Ace), 4 Shanks, 3 Yasopp, 4 Otama, 2 Samurai, ≥3 Decken. The spell only works if you already have attackers on board; best target = their early 1-drops (denies their Law).
3. Full starve Sabo and Enel (switch to face when Sabo goes double 6-cost), board control Rocks and Robin (Oden the Captain John; take the first two banishes and swing 5-5), take first vs Kaido and open on Yasopps, race Luffy-Ace at an odd life total, vs Boa never hit face early and double clear.
4. Counter rules: take the first two life in the mirror and defend the third; defend a body only if it board-controls them back next turn or costs one card; vs Robin always take the first banish.
5. From behind: read your hand, pick the one win condition (Shanks race / board control / push), envision the world where they miss — "if they had the pieces, you weren't winning anyway".
6. Weekend: bed an hour early (the mind needs an hour to calm down; stressors on paper), caffeine after an hour awake and again at halfway, snacks, water bottle; write down the bad losses and move on; work backwards from the winning board.
