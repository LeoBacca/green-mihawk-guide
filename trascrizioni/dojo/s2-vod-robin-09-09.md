# OP17 MIHAWK VS ROBIN

### Matchup Overview

Mihawk versus Robin is a race between two very different resource engines. Mihawk wants to establish a wide, active board, pressure Robin with repeated 7k attacks, and use Shanks plus Luna to deny Robin’s defensive turns. Robin wants to ramp quickly, develop Teach and Big Mom ahead of schedule, heal through the early pressure, and eventually force Mihawk to fight through Giant-backed bodies and multiple large bodies.

The main lesson from the session is that the matchup is heavily shaped by **tempo before the first 10-cost Big Mom turn**.

- Mihawk wins when it pressures Robin before Robin can establish multiple large bodies and Giant defense.
- Robin wins when it ramps efficiently, gets to Teach into Big Mom before Mihawk’s board is online, and keeps enough Giant or counter access to prevent Shanks from removing the first major body.
- Mihawk should usually prefer **going first** to deny Robin’s most explosive going-second ramp curve.
- Robin generally prefers **going second** because its strongest starts involve ramping, then reaching its high-cost threats ahead of schedule.

## Mihawk’s Matchup Plan

### Take Second

The session’s strongest strategic conclusion is that Mihawk should generally choose second against Robin.

Why:

- Robin’s most dangerous high roll occurs going second.
- Going second allows Robin to double-ramp early.
- That ramp can lead into 8-cost Teach followed by 10-cost Big Mom.
- Mihawk is much less comfortable when Robin’s first Big Mom arrives before Mihawk has built enough board or assembled Shanks plus Luna.

The matchup is still playable from other die-roll positions, but Mihawk’s first-player seat is much better for controlling Robin’s progression.

### Pressure Robin Before Big Mom

Mihawk cannot sit back and let Robin build a full healing board.

Robin’s leader banishes life rather than giving Mihawk cards, but Robin pays a real cost for its early setup:

- It often discards cards for leader skill.
- It may discard cards to ramp.
- It has to spend cards to find Pudding, Teach, Big Mom, and Giant.
- Its early hand can become much smaller than it appears.
- If Mihawk starts sending repeated 7k attacks, Robin may be forced to spend cards before its larger threats stabilize.

The core Mihawk plan is:

1. Develop bodies early.
2. Push real attack thresholds.
3. Force Robin to answer 7k attacks.
4. Keep Robin from freely ramping into Teach plus Big Mom.
5. Go wide enough that Robin’s first defensive turn cannot answer everything.
6. Use Shanks plus Luna once Robin is low enough that the freeze actually matters.

Impact’s central observation was that Robin’s board, without Giant and without enough open DON!!, often folds to repeated 7k attacks. Robin can heal, but it cannot defend every attack if Mihawk is consistently asking for multiple cards.

### Attack Sizing Against Robin

#### Repeated 7k attacks are the core pressure tool

Mihawk should use 7k attacks to create the most difficult defensive decisions.

Why 7k matters:

- Robin’s leader cannot efficiently cheat defense without Giant.
- Robin often has limited natural 2k density after ramping and developing.
- A 7k attack often asks for 2k plus 1k, two cards, a defensive event, or life.
- Robin may be forced to take damage before it has a good healing turn.
- Multiple 7k attacks drain Robin’s hand before Big Mom can restore the board.

Mihawk should not default to:

- 5k leader swings.
- Small attacks that Robin can take comfortably.
- One giant attack that Robin can Giant through efficiently.
- Board trades that do not reduce Robin’s actual future pressure.

The preferred goal is not merely “hit life.” It is to make Robin spend the exact cards it needs for its ramp, Teach, Big Mom, and Giant sequence.

#### Use extra DON!! productively

If Mihawk has extra DON!! and does not need it for:

- Rad Beam
- Another defensive event
- Samurai
- Boat
- Searcher cycling
- A needed development play

then it should convert the DON!! into a larger attack.

A 5k attack into Robin can be low impact. A 7k attack can force a real card exchange and accelerate the game before Robin’s healing engine comes online.

## Robin’s Matchup Plan

### Robin Wants the Game to Reach Big Mom

Robin’s goal is not to trade evenly in the early turns. It wants to ramp into large bodies fast enough that Mihawk’s normal midgame attacks become inefficient.

Robin’s best sequence is generally:

1. Ramp.
2. Develop Pudding or another card-selection piece.
3. Get to Teach.
4. Follow with 10-cost Big Mom.
5. Heal.
6. Hold Giant or establish Borsalino.
7. Force Mihawk to spend Shanks, Vander Decken, and Luna just to get through one defensive turn.
8. Develop a second Big Mom or Sweet Generals while Mihawk runs out of pressure.

The most dangerous Robin high roll is a fast curve such as:

- Double-ramp early.
- Teach on the 6-DON!! turn.
- Big Mom on the next major curve.
- Trigger value or healing support.
- Giant-backed board protection.

If this happens while Mihawk misses 6-cost development, misses useful one-drops, or gets Samurai stuck in hand, Mihawk can be overwhelmed.

### Do Not Counter Every Banish Attack

Robin’s banish leader forces an unusual decision pattern. The opponent does not gain a card when taking life, so it is tempting to counter every banish attack.

The session’s conclusion was that this is often wrong.

Against Robin, Mihawk should usually:

- Take at least one early banish attack when safe.
- Preserve hand for board development and defense.
- Counter later banish swings when the life threshold becomes important.
- Avoid spending every card just to remain at high life.
- Prioritize surviving the Big Mom and Giant turns rather than preserving early life at all costs.

The reason is simple:

- If Mihawk counters every banish swing, Robin can focus on clearing Mihawk’s board.
- Mihawk loses cards protecting life.
- Robin gets more time to ramp.
- Mihawk enters the critical turns with too little board and too little hand.

Banish attacks matter, but board is still the deciding resource.

## The Robin High Roll

### Double Ramp Into Teach

Mihawk must understand Robin’s strongest curve because it changes the entire matchup.

Robin’s ideal high-roll progression can include:

- Early ramp.
- A second ramp or trigger-assisted acceleration.
- Teach on 6 DON!!.
- Fast 10-cost Big Mom.
- Healing or trigger support.
- Giant access.

This is the sequence Mihawk should fear most.

Why it is dangerous:

- Teach interferes with Mihawk’s board and hand progression.
- Big Mom is difficult to attack through.
- Robin can heal back before Mihawk finishes.
- Giant can absorb the attack that would otherwise remove Big Mom.
- Mihawk may have to spend Shanks and Vander Decken too early.
- Robin can then establish a second large body while Mihawk is low on pressure.

### How Mihawk responds

Mihawk must punish the setup before it becomes complete.

The best tools are:

- Early 7k pressure.
- Yasopp to remove Robin’s 4k bodies.
- Oden to freeze the character that makes the next turn dangerous.
- Perona to reduce the number of attacks Robin can make.
- Wider development rather than relying on one Shanks turn.
- Stun Bonney to deny a critical 10-DON!! turn when the board state supports it.
- Dead Man’s Game, if included, to tap Robin’s DON!! and shut off Giant defense.

## Dead Man’s Game

### Why DMG may become mandatory

The session identifies Dead Man’s Game as a potentially important future inclusion if Robin becomes popular.

Robin’s strongest defensive turns frequently involve:

- Holding Giant.
- Holding one or more DON!! open.
- Playing Borsalino.
- Using Giant to save Big Mom.
- Rebuilding through triggers or Sweet Generals.

Dead Man’s Game can break that structure.

If Mihawk taps Robin’s key DON!!:

- Giant may become unavailable.
- Borsalino plus Giant lines become weaker.
- Robin cannot protect Big Mom as efficiently.
- Shanks becomes much harder to answer.
- A second Shanks can often finish the game immediately.

The group’s view was that one Dead Man’s Game can drastically change games that otherwise look unwinnable.

#### Important DMG principle

DMG is not just a generic event. Against Robin, it is often a direct answer to the deck’s survival mechanism.

The goal is:

1. Force Robin low.
2. Identify a turn where Giant is the only real defense.
3. Play Shanks.
4. Tap the necessary DON!!.
5. Push the board and leader attacks.
6. Leave Robin without the Giant line that would normally buy another turn.

## Shanks Plus Luna

### When to use the package

Mihawk should not automatically Shanks plus Luna at the first possible opportunity.

The package is strongest when:

- Robin is at one or zero life.
- Robin’s board is rested or vulnerable to the full freeze.
- Robin cannot hold Giant effectively.
- Mihawk has already forced Robin to use much of its hand.
- Mihawk can kill on the current or following turn.
- Robin’s attackers would otherwise threaten to stabilize through Sweet Generals or Big Mom.

The ideal sequence is:

1. Use your existing board first.
2. Make as many meaningful attacks as possible.
3. Force Robin to reveal triggers, blocks, or counter.
4. Play Shanks.
5. Rest the board.
6. Luna after the Shanks resolves.
7. Freeze Robin’s relevant attackers.
8. Push Robin to zero or leave it unable to defend the next Shanks.

### Why sequence matters

The session highlights that attacks should generally happen before Shanks when possible.

If Mihawk attacks first:

- Robin may trigger bodies.
- Robin may reveal whether it has Giant.
- Robin may reduce hand size.
- Mihawk can see which bodies are active before Shanks rests everything.
- Luna becomes more effective once Shanks has created the proper board state.

The basic sequence is: Attack first, then Shanks, then Luna.

Do not waste the opportunity to make your existing board attack before freezing the opponent’s side.

## Mihawk Development Strategy

### Go wide, not tall

Mihawk’s strongest plan into Robin is frequently to develop more bodies rather than race to Shanks immediately.

Why:

- Robin’s first Big Mom can answer one major threat.
- Robin’s Sweet Generals can interact with individual bodies.
- Giant can protect one decisive target.
- A wide Mihawk board asks Robin to solve several problems at once.
- Multiple 6k and 7k attackers can force Robin’s hand down before Big Mom becomes safe.

A development pattern such as:

- 6-cost plus 5-cost
- 6-cost plus 5-cost again
- Perona plus another body
- Law plus Perona
- Oden plus board pressure

can be better than simply jamming Shanks at the first opportunity.

The goal is to create a board where Robin cannot:

- Heal enough.
- Clear enough attackers.
- Protect Big Mom.
- Maintain Giant.
- Develop the second Big Mom.
- Keep enough cards for banish pressure.

### Mihawk before Law

The session also reinforces a sequencing principle for lists playing both Mihawk and Law:

> Play Mihawk first, then Law on the following turn.

Why:

- Mihawk’s body enters active.
- Law’s summoned body enters rested.
- If Mihawk is played first, the board’s bodies are more likely to become rested together on the later Law turn.
- This makes the board easier to manage around opponent attack patterns.
- It also reduces the chance of leaving one isolated body as an easy target.

This is not an absolute rule, but it is a useful sequencing default.

## Perona and Oden

### Perona’s role

Perona is valuable against Robin because it can reduce the number of meaningful attacks Robin sends while also creating a resilient board body.

Perona is especially good when:

- Robin has multiple small or medium characters.
- Robin is about to play a Big Mom but has limited board.
- Freezing one key body means Big Mom only gets one weak follow-up attack.
- Mihawk needs to survive the turn before Shanks.
- Mihawk needs to keep Robin’s board narrow.

### Oden’s role

Oden is valuable when one character is the real problem.

Use Oden when:

- Robin has a major attacker that must not swing.
- Mihawk needs to stop the most threatening body before the next Shanks turn.
- The board is narrow and freezing one body materially reduces pressure.
- Mihawk needs the draw or filter to find combo pieces.

The main difference:

- Perona is better at reducing a wide board.
- Oden is better at stopping the one character that decides the turn.

## One-Drop Hate and Mont-dor

### Do not play around it by not playing your deck

Robin may include one-drop hate such as Mont-dor. This can punish Mihawk’s one-drop development and cause a blowout if it removes multiple searchers or Samurai targets.

However, the session’s conclusion is clear: Mihawk should not stop playing one-drops just because Robin could have Mont-dor.

Why:

- Mihawk’s deck functions through cycling, searching, and Samurai.
- Refusing to play one-drops means refusing to use your engine.
- Robin has to spend a real turn and card on Mont-dor.
- If Robin plays it, it delays ramp or another key development.
- The card is a blowout when it happens, but not something Mihawk should build its entire game around.

The correct response is to acknowledge the risk, not cripple your own curve trying to avoid it.

## Robin’s Defensive Tools

### Big Mom

Big Mom is Robin’s central stabilizer.

Against Big Mom, Mihawk must ask:

- Does Robin have Giant?
- Does Robin have open DON!!?
- Can Shanks plus Vander Decken remove it?
- Can a wide board pressure Robin around Big Mom instead?
- Is it better to freeze the supporting board and race?
- Can Dead Man’s Game prevent Giant?

Mihawk should not assume Big Mom is removable immediately. If Giant is live, attacking into Big Mom may simply trade Mihawk’s biggest attack for Robin’s best defensive event.

### Borsalino plus Giant

Borsalino plus Giant is one of Robin’s best defensive lines.

It can:

- Preserve life.
- Keep a major body standing.
- Stop the largest attack.
- Force Mihawk to commit more resources.
- Buy the turn needed for another Big Mom, Sweet Generals, or heal.

Mihawk’s response is to avoid allowing Robin to enter this defensive posture with a real attacking board.

If Robin has:

- Borsalino
- Giant
- A major body
- Multiple active attackers

then Mihawk can be in danger.

If Robin has:

- Borsalino
- Giant
- No real attacking board

then Robin is only delaying. Mihawk can continue developing, pressing life, and wait for the right Shanks or DMG turn.

## Life Management

### Mihawk Life

Mihawk should not automatically preserve life against Robin.

Take early banish hits when:

- Your hand needs cards to develop.
- You can survive the future Shanks / Big Mom sequence.
- Robin has not built enough attackers to punish low life.
- You need to preserve counter for a key body.
- You need to establish a board before Robin ramps.

Counter when:

- Robin can deal multiple large attacks in the same turn.
- You need to stay above a known lethal threshold.
- A future Big Mom or Sweet Generals turn will create an unavoidable attack spread.
- You are protecting a critical body or Shanks turn.
- Your hand already has the curve and does not need more life cards.

### Robin Life

Mihawk should focus on getting Robin to one or zero before the full healing engine begins.

Robin can recover life, but every heal has a cost:

- A card must be played.
- DON!! must be committed.
- Counter may be reduced.
- Giant may become necessary.

Repeated 7k attacks make Robin choose between:

- Taking life and losing access to a card.
- Countering with cards needed for future development.
- Healing but falling behind on board.
- Protecting Big Mom but losing the board around it.

The goal is to make every defensive choice bad.

## Mulligan Considerations

### Mihawk Mulligan

A hand is not good simply because it has Samurai and a one-drop.

Against Robin, Mihawk wants:

- Multiple one-drop searchers.
- Kin’emon, Otama, and Perona access.
- Oden or Perona for midgame interaction.
- A functional 5-cost or 6-cost development line.
- A route to Shanks.
- Enough bodies to build a board before Big Mom.
- At least some card flow that does not rely entirely on one Otama hit.

A hand containing one one-drop plus Samurai can still be a mulligan if everything depends on Otama finding exactly the missing body. The hand may look structurally close, but it is not functional enough if one search whiff means the whole curve fails.

### Robin Mulligan

Robin wants to prioritize:

- Ramp.
- Pudding.
- Teach.
- Big Mom access.
- Giant.
- A route to a second major body.
- Enough counter or trigger access to survive early 7k pressure.

Robin cannot keep a hand that only looks good on the 10-DON!! turn. It needs a functional early progression because Mihawk’s 7k pressure can make an unprotected Big Mom turn too late.

## Common Mistakes

### Mihawk Mistakes

- Taking first and allowing Robin its best double-ramp curve.
- Keeping a hand that needs one searcher to hit perfectly.
- Attacking Robin only for 5k when unused DON!! could create a 7k threshold.
- Overcommitting to an early Shanks without putting Robin in lethal range.
- Using Luna too early, before Robin is under enough pressure for the turn skip to matter.
- Trying to avoid Mont-dor by refusing to play one-drops.
- Failing to include or test Dead Man’s Game if Robin becomes a major part of the field.
- Treating Big Mom as the only relevant target instead of reducing Robin’s attacking board first.
- Ignoring whether Giant is live.
- Spending hand to protect life while Robin clears board and reaches Big Mom safely.

### Robin Mistakes

- Countering every early attack and losing too many cards before Big Mom.
- Ramping aggressively without tracking the hand cost.
- Playing Big Mom without Giant or enough counter when Mihawk has Shanks access.
- Using Banish pressure while failing to remove Mihawk’s board.
- Turning medium characters into Mihawk’s board without forcing meaningful exchanges.
- Letting Mihawk build five attackers before attempting the first major defensive turn.
- Playing Mont-dor as a default line instead of recognizing when it delays a more important ramp or development turn.
- Treating a full healing board as enough to win when Mihawk still has multiple attacks and a Shanks turn coming.

## Tournament Preparation

### Mihawk Player Practice Goals

Practice these scenarios:

1. Going first versus Robin double-ramp starts.
2. Pressure lines that create repeated 7k attacks before Teach.
3. Shanks plus Luna sequencing when Robin is at one life.
4. Dead Man’s Game timing into Giant.
5. Perona versus Oden decisions when Robin has narrow versus wide board.
6. Playing around Big Mom plus Giant without overcommitting Shanks.
7. Recovering when Mont-dor clears several early one-drops.
8. Deciding whether to take banish life or protect board.

### Robin Player Practice Goals

Practice these scenarios:

1. Double-ramp into Teach versus early Mihawk 7k pressure.
2. Big Mom turns with and without Giant.
3. Borsalino plus Giant defensive sequencing.
4. When to take 7k attacks versus countering.
5. When to use character attacks to clear Mihawk board rather than push banish life.
6. Building a second Big Mom board before Mihawk’s second Shanks.
7. Playing around Dead Man’s Game.
8. Managing hand size after leader effect, ramp, and Pudding lines.

## Final Takeaways

- **Mihawk should generally choose second** to reduce Robin’s double-ramp high roll.
- **Robin must ramp quickly**, but excessive ramp can leave it too low on hand to survive repeated 7k attacks.
- **Mihawk’s strongest plan is wide pressure**, not simply rushing Shanks into the first Big Mom.
- **7k attacks matter** because Robin cannot efficiently defend them without Giant or spending cards it needs to stabilize.
- **Do not counter every banish hit.** Mihawk needs hand for development, events, and board protection.
- **Shanks plus Luna should be used when Robin is already under lethal pressure**, not simply because the combo is available.
- **Dead Man’s Game may become a key Robin tech** because tapping DON!! can turn off Giant and make Big Mom removable.
- **Mont-dor is a real blowout but should not stop Mihawk from playing its one-drop engine.**
- **Robin’s best defense is Big Mom plus Giant plus a real board.** If Robin is only defending without attackers, Mihawk can continue developing and eventually overwhelm it.
- **The matchup is won before the second Big Mom.** Mihawk wants Robin low and short on hand before that point; Robin wants to survive long enough to create a healing wall Mihawk cannot efficiently break.
