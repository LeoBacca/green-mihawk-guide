## IMPACT OP17 META Q&A

### Deck Selection, Meta Updates, Matchup Theory, and Tournament Preparation

### Session Overview

This session is Impact responding to Dojo questions while updating his view of the OP17 metagame shortly before the weekend's major events. The conversation centers on Mihawk, Robin, Enel, Sabo, Rocks, Luffy Ace, deck selection, new tech choices, and how to prepare mentally for long tournament days.

The largest update is that **Mihawk’s new rest-event build has improved its early-game control matchups substantially**. Impact believes the deck can now remove early Sabo and Enel bodies earlier and more efficiently than before, which makes Sabo a much less attractive registration than it was in prior weeks.

His broad current hierarchy is:

- **Mihawk**
- **Robin**
- **Enel**
- **Red-Green Luffy Ace**

He sees these as the most realistic high-level event choices. Rocks remains extremely powerful into much of the field, but its Mihawk matchup is too severe for Impact to recommend it if Mihawk remains popular.

## Meta Update

### Mihawk Has Improved

Impact believes Mihawk became better because the new rest spell gives the deck earlier, cheaper board control.

Previously, Mihawk often had to wait until Yasopp or later interaction to answer early characters. That gave decks such as Sabo and Enel opportunities to establish an early board and leverage it before Mihawk’s main control tools came online.

Now, Mihawk can:

1. Play stage.
2. Develop a one-drop.
3. Use the rest spell on the following turn.
4. Rest an opposing early body.
5. Attack it for a meaningful number.
6. Draw or filter while trading favorably.

This changes the exchange dramatically.

If Mihawk uses one card that replaces itself and removes the opponent’s character:

- Mihawk often loses no meaningful hand resources.
- The opponent either loses the character outright or spends multiple cards defending it.
- Mihawk gains time to develop Yasopp, Oden, Perona, Law, and Shanks.

Impact sees this as especially important against Sabo and Enel, which previously gained a lot of equity from early board development.

### Sabo Is Worse This Week

Impact is much lower on Sabo than he was previously.

Why:

- Mihawk’s rest spell gives early answers to Sabo’s key 2-cost development.
- Mihawk can now rest and remove a Sabo body before it becomes a 6k attacker.
- Yasopp can remove the next body.
- The rest spell can later remove Saul or another important Giant.
- Sabo loses much of its early pressure if its first two meaningful bodies are removed.

Impact’s conclusion is blunt: Registering Sabo into the current Mihawk-focused field is likely to be a bad experience.

Sabo can still win when it curves perfectly or Mihawk fails to find interaction, but the matchup has shifted away from the early OP17 Sabo advantage.

## Choosing a Deck

### Mihawk vs Sabo

Impact’s answer to the question “Why register Sabo with practice instead of Mihawk with OP16.5 experience?” is effectively that there is little reason to choose Sabo right now unless a player has extraordinary comfort with it and expects a very specific field.

Mihawk is the better general registration because:

- It remains highly consistent.
- It rewards strong fundamentals.
- It gained new tools against previous difficult matchups.
- It has a good plan into Sabo, Enel, Robin, and Rocks.
- The mirror is a fundamentals matchup where OP16.5 experience still transfers.
- It is less dependent on the opponent failing to interact with its board.

### Play the Deck You Know

Impact’s event recommendation is not to chase every new list or switch decks because of fear of missing out.

For a major event:

- Pick the deck you understand.
- Know your matchup plan.
- Know your mulligans.
- Know your ideal curve.
- Know your expected lethal patterns.
- Use the same deck throughout the event unless you have a clear reason to switch.

He sees very little value in changing leaders between different portions of an event if you are less practiced with the alternate deck.

Comfort, familiarity, and matchup knowledge matter more than chasing a theoretical “perfect” list at the last minute.

## Mihawk vs Rocks

### Matchup Assessment

Impact views Mihawk versus Rocks as very favorable, but warns that players must understand why.

Mihawk’s major advantages:

- Mihawk is a 6k leader.
- Rocks must often commit DON!! to make its leader attacks meaningful.
- If Rocks spends DON!! attacking, it can fall off curve.
- Mihawk can remove or freeze the characters that make Rocks’ board function.
- Mihawk can use Shanks, Vander Decken, Yasopp, Perona, Oden, and stuns to control board.
- Rocks struggles if Newgate and Shiki cannot remain in play.
- Once Mihawk wins board, Rocks’ non-counter cards become much worse.

### Do Not Pure Race

Impact identifies two possible Mihawk plans:

#### Face pressure plus Luna

Mihawk can attack life, push Rocks low, then Luna the board.

The danger is Captain John plus Usopp spell.

Captain John plus Usopp spell can function like Rocks “Luna-ing Mihawk back”:

- Rocks gets an extra turn.
- Mihawk cannot efficiently kill the board.
- Rocks can repeat pressure.
- The matchup can become dangerous if Rocks has more Captain John and Usopp spell access than Mihawk has answers.

#### Board control first

Impact prefers board control.

The plan:

1. Remove Newgate, Shiki, Linlin, and other Rocks engines.
2. Use Yasopp plus Vander Decken to kill Shiki.
3. Use Shanks as board control, not merely face damage.
4. Use Oden to stop Captain John from resting again.
5. Prevent Captain John plus Usopp spell from generating repeated turns.
6. Once Rocks has no engine board, pressure life and finish quickly.

The main point is that Rocks cannot win without a board. It has cards in hand, but its hand only becomes defense, pressure, and value when Newgate, Shiki, and similar characters are in play.

### Captain John Rule

When Rocks uses Captain John plus Usopp spell:

- Do not panic and race blindly.
- Get cards out of hand where possible.
- Use Oden to freeze Captain John or the body that enables the next pressure turn.
- Ensure Rocks cannot reuse the same Captain John for a second major turn.
- Remove its other attackers so Captain John does not become a real threat.

Impact compares this to older Kid matchups. Keeping one protected unit alive is not enough if every other attacker is gone.

## Mihawk vs Sabo

### Matchup Assessment

Impact now strongly prefers the rest spell build into Sabo.

The Mihawk plan is:

1. Starve Sabo early.
2. Use the rest spell to remove its first 2-cost body.
3. Use Yasopp to remove the next body.
4. Use the rest spell or other interaction to remove Saul.
5. Keep Sabo from developing a Giant-enabled board.
6. Establish your own board advantage.
7. Switch to life pressure only after you have won board.

This is a much different plan than blindly racing.

### Why Starving Works

Sabo needs life, board, and card access to create a critical mass of 2-cost bodies plus Giant effects.

If Mihawk starves Sabo while removing early bodies:

- Sabo cannot establish its normal pressure board.
- Sabo cannot comfortably play its Luffy turns.
- Saul or Loki becomes less impactful.
- Its board lacks enough attackers to race.
- Mihawk can develop while Sabo is forced to keep replacing pieces.

The goal is not to starve forever. The goal is to starve until Mihawk has a board lead. Once that happens, Mihawk transitions into a two-turn kill.

### When to Change to Face

Impact’s practical trigger is when Sabo reaches a double-6-cost development or starts playing bodies that survive the early-control plan.

At that point:

- Mihawk likely has a board advantage.
- One of Mihawk’s bodies probably survived.
- Mihawk can start chaining Oden into Sabo’s larger threats.
- Mihawk can begin attacking life while still freezing or controlling board.
- Sabo’s hand should already be limited from replacing early bodies.

The rest spell is strongest before Sabo’s board becomes active. Once Sabo has a larger board, Mihawk must use its usual Odin, Yasopp, Perona, Law, and Shanks tools to finish the game.

## Mihawk vs Robin

### Matchup Assessment

Impact sees Robin as slightly favored, approximately 55-45, but believes the matchup is playable if Mihawk approaches it as a gradual board-control game instead of a pure race.

Robin’s strengths:

- Banish leader pressure.
- Ramp.
- Teach.
- Sweet Generals.
- Big Mom.
- Giant.
- Borsalino.
- Large hand and healing late game.

Mihawk’s plan is to prevent Robin from having a board when its Big Mom turns begin.

### Early Game

Mihawk should:

- Use Yasopp heavily.
- Remove Robin’s early 4k bodies.
- Keep Robin’s board narrow.
- Avoid turning your own bodies into Sweet Generals trades when Robin has board.
- Take the first one or two banish hits when needed to preserve hand for board defense.
- Use smaller 5k attacks to pressure Robin’s low early hand.

Robin often spends several cards ramping:

- Leader effect costs cards.
- Ramp costs cards.
- Pudding requires hand investment.
- Teach, Big Mom, Giant, and heal lines require resources.

Mihawk should punish this with multiple small attacks rather than one big attack Robin can take and heal.

### When to Attack Life

Attack life when:

- Robin’s hand is low.
- Robin has ramped and is short on counter.
- Mihawk has already controlled the board.
- Robin cannot easily play a Big Mom plus Giant turn.
- Multiple 5k attacks force multiple small counters.
- Robin is approaching one or zero life before a healing turn.

Impact prefers several 5k swings with smaller bodies, such as Perona and Kin’emon, over one giant leader swing when Robin is low on cards. Multiple attacks force Robin to make more decisions and may prevent it from comfortably healing back.

### Shanks as Board Control

Against Robin, Shanks should often be used as a board-control tool.

Strong Shanks lines include:

- Rest Robin’s board.
- Use Mihawk bodies to attack smaller rested characters.
- Vander Decken Big Mom.
- Attack Teach with Shanks.
- Force Giant while Robin also loses two or three cards.
- Leave Robin with a slow next turn and insufficient hand to defend.

Mihawk does not need to kill Big Mom immediately in every game. It needs to make Robin’s Big Mom turn weak.

If Robin’s Big Mom is only a 10-DON!! heal and draw with no active attackers behind it, Mihawk is in a much better position.

### Law Is Excellent

Impact considers Law very strong against Robin.

Law can:

- Recycle Vander Decken.
- Recycle Perona.
- Create a blocker.
- Attack when Robin has no efficient body to contest it.
- Create repeated board-control turns.
- Help Mihawk maintain wide development.
- Help prevent Robin from establishing a second full Big Mom board.

## Mihawk vs Kaido

### Matchup Assessment

Impact considers Kaido a very favorable Mihawk matchup.

Mihawk should usually take first.

Why:

- Kaido wants time and curve space.
- Kaido wants the easy King ramp line.
- Taking first interferes with Kaido’s preferred ramp progression.
- Mihawk gets an extra attacker into play.
- Mihawk can begin life pressure before Kaido reaches repeated boss turns.

### Key Plan

1. Develop Yasopp early.
2. Use Yasopp to avoid Kaido leader interaction because it stands again.
3. Pressure Kaido’s life early.
4. Use Oden to stun King.
5. Force Kaido to use leader effect and counter before Shanks.
6. Use Shanks plus Vander Decken to answer Kaido’s major bodies.
7. Continue pressing because Kaido can heal, so plan for one extra turn.

### Attack Sizing

Against Kaido, attack sizes can manipulate leader effect.

- Use 6k attacks to bait Kaido leader effect.
- Use 7k attacks when you want Kaido to spend leader effect plus a card.
- If Kaido spends leader effect on a smaller attack, it becomes harder for it to protect a major body later.
- Once leader effect is spent, Shanks can threaten a large character with 17k or more.

The goal is to overload Kaido’s DON!!. Kaido may need to:

- Develop.
- Heal.
- Use leader effect.
- Defend life.
- Protect a big body.
- Answer Mihawk’s board.

It cannot always do all of those things in one turn.

## Mihawk Build Choice

### Rest Spell Build

Impact is leaning toward the rest spell build for major events.

The rest spell build is better into:

- Enel.
- Sabo.
- Robin.
- Early aggressive decks.
- Random ladder or tournament decks.
- Fields where board control is more valuable than mirror-only optimization.

The rest spell lets Mihawk:

- Rest early characters.
- Use free DON!! to remove them.
- Deny early development.
- Keep opponents off key curves.
- Board control before they build a functional attack plan.
- Survive Shanks pressure through flexible event defense.

Impact sees it as more than a single tech card. It is a full game plan.

If you include it, commit to the strategy:

- Use it repeatedly.
- Build board so you can attack the rested character.
- Do not spend the spell as generic removal while behind.
- Use it to establish a lead, then attack with five 7k swings once you have enough board.

### Perona / Midrange Build

Impact also likes the Perona-heavy midrange version.

It is better when:

- You expect many Mihawk mirrors.
- You want stronger 7k body density.
- You want more resilient board development.
- You want Perona’s protection against rest-based board control.
- You want more direct pressure through 8k leader swings and Shanks turns.

This build is more mirror-focused, while the rest spell build is more broad-field focused.

### Hybrid Build

Impact sees hybrid builds as viable.

A likely hybrid direction:

- 3 Law.
- 2 Mihawk.
- 3 Yasopp.
- 3 Perona.
- 3 Vander Decken.
- Shanks plus Law/Bepo split.
- A mix of Rad Beam, Stronger, DMG, and rest spells based on field expectations.

He considers the 3 Law, 2 Mihawk configuration preferable to a 4 Law, 1 Mihawk structure if rest spells are common. More Mihawks reduce dependence on one-drops, which can be removed by early rest-spell lines.

## Event Choices

### Stronger vs Luna

Impact prefers Stronger over Luna in rest-spell builds.

Why:

- Stronger is a more flexible defensive event.
- With four stages, the deck can often cast a spell cheaply or effectively for free.
- Stronger can stop several 7k attacks.
- Luna can be dead outside certain matchups.
- Rest spell plus board control can reduce the number of bodies that need to be frozen.
- In many Sabo and Luffy Ace situations, freezing two important attackers is enough.

Luna is still strong in dedicated mirror or Shanks-focused lists. But Impact sees Stronger as the more flexible choice for an all-field rest-spell build.

### Dead Man’s Game

Impact rates Dead Man’s Game highly.

It is useful because it:

- Taps Robin’s Giant DON!!.
- Taps open DON!! in a Shanks plus Decken/Luna turn where opponent has one DON!! up.
- Helps force lethal against Robin.
- Can deny a key event line.
- Is valuable in mirrors where the opponent needs one DON!! for defense or development.
- Helps push through a blocker plus open DON!! scenario.

Impact considers DMG especially strong when Mihawk has already won board. Tapping the final defensive DON!! or Giant resource can make the opponent’s entire defensive turn collapse.

### Four Shanks vs Three Shanks Plus Law/Bepo

Impact prefers four Shanks.

His reasoning:

- Shanks is the deck’s strongest win condition.
- Seeing Shanks plus Shanks is better than seeing Shanks plus Law/Bepo in most matchups.
- The deck can use Shanks to create immediate damage, board control, and lethal pressure.
- Law/Bepo is useful as a turn extension, but may be less important than a fourth copy of the main finisher.
- The more aggressive Mihawk build especially wants four Shanks.

Law/Bepo remains playable as a one-of because it can:

- Make Mihawk an 8k leader.
- Pseudo-blank opposing 6k bodies.
- Freeze a large attacker.
- Buy an extra turn.
- Create a 10k to 11k leader swing.
- Bridge into the second Shanks turn.

But Impact is not convinced the 3 Shanks, 1 Law/Bepo split is stronger than simply playing four Shanks.

## Mihawk vs Enel

### Updated View: Starve and Control

Impact has changed his opinion on Mihawk versus Enel.

He now believes Mihawk should often **starve Enel**, control board, and make Enel run out of removal.

The plan:

1. Do not feed Enel easy life cards.
2. Use rest spell, Yasopp, Perona, Oden, and Vander Decken to control Enel’s board.
3. Force Enel to spend removal spells.
4. Do not give Enel more cards while it is trying to clear your board.
5. Eventually stick one or two meaningful bodies.
6. Play Shanks and remove Enel’s big threat.
7. Force Enel to take life in one large board turn.
8. Kill over the next one or two turns.

Why the plan works:

- Enel’s removal spells do not replace themselves.
- If Mihawk starves Enel, Enel must find both removal and a character repeatedly.
- Enel may be forced to spend hand to remove Mihawk’s board without receiving new life cards.
- Once Mihawk sticks multiple bodies, Enel cannot efficiently clear all of them.

Mihawk may go low in life while starving Enel, but that is acceptable if Enel is also running out of cards and cannot maintain its board.

## Playing From Behind

### Hand Context Determines the Plan

Impact explains that Mihawk should not play every game the same way.

When behind or when the opener is weak, determine the plan from the hand.

#### Shanks-heavy hand

If the hand contains multiple Shanks but lacks a strong early engine:

- Play aggressively.
- Send damage face.
- Aim for a Shanks-based race.
- Take calculated risks.
- Force the opponent to have the specific answers.
- Accept that the plan loses to more things, because the standard resource game was already losing.

#### Midrange-heavy hand

If the hand contains multiple 5-costs, 6-costs, Yasopps, Peronas, or board-control pieces:

- Play a board-control game.
- Remove one of the opponent’s double-development bodies.
- Reduce the opponent’s attack count.
- Make their double summon less impactful.
- Stabilize, then convert board advantage into life pressure.

#### No-searcher hand

If Mihawk misses searchers while the opponent finds many:

- You are unlikely to win a pure card-quality battle.
- Look for a line where the opponent misses a key answer.
- Push more aggressively.
- Identify your actual possible win condition.
- Do not pretend you can beat every possible hand.
- Make the play that beats the widest realistic range.

Impact’s principle: When you are behind, play toward the line that wins if the opponent does not have the exact answer. If they have every answer, you were not winning anyway.

## Tournament Mental Game

### Sleep

Impact emphasizes sleeping earlier than you think you need to.

His recommendation:

- Go to bed about one hour before your actual intended sleep time.
- Give your mind time to settle.
- Do not expect to instantly sleep after a stressful practice session or travel day.
- Write down stressful thoughts, tasks, and tournament concerns before bed.
- Telling yourself “I will revisit this later” helps the mind stop looping on it.

### Caffeine and Food

For long tournament days:

- Do not caffeinate immediately after waking up.
- Wait roughly an hour until you are naturally awake.
- Take caffeine strategically.
- Consider a second caffeine point around the middle of the day.
- Carry water.
- Stay hydrated.
- Bring small snacks.
- Maintain stable energy rather than relying on one huge meal or one early caffeine spike.

Impact treats hydration and food as tournament preparation, not an afterthought. Dehydration can hurt performance as much as poor sleep.

### Handling Losses

When a bad beat, misplay, or frustrating game happens:

1. Write down what happened.
2. Note the turn or decision that bothered you.
3. Promise yourself you will review it later.
4. Return focus to the next round.

Do not let the brain replay a loss all day. Writing it down gives you permission to stop thinking about it until the event is over.

### Playing Pace

A long event is a marathon.

Impact’s guidance:

- Do not rush because you are excited, nervous, or behind.
- Play at an appropriate pace.
- Think through turns.
- Avoid slow play, but do not donate games by making fast mistakes.
- Work backward from the winning board state.
- Use searches to take the cards that support that endgame.

## Finals and National-Level Events

### What Changes at Finals

Impact believes finals-level events are somewhat more serious than a normal regional, but not dramatically different in baseline player quality.

Expect:

- Fewer completely unprepared opponents.
- More players who can win locals.
- More top meta decks.
- Fewer random rogue decks.
- More Mihawk, Robin, Enel, Luffy Ace, and other established good decks.

Do not assume every opponent is a top-cut regional-level expert. Players can qualify through store championships, so the field still includes a broad skill range.

The practical difference is that “round one random rogue deck” is less likely. Tournament preparation should prioritize high-play-rate decks and established Tier 1 choices.

### Playing on Stream or at Top Tables

Impact’s mental approach is to treat spectators and stream conditions as white noise.

His rule: The only thing that matters is playing the current match as well as possible.

He attributes this partly to esports experience. He does not attempt to manage the audience. He narrows attention to:

- Board state.
- Hand.
- Life.
- DON!!.
- Matchup plan.
- Next-turn lethal and survival math.

## Final Meta Conclusions

Impact’s current OP17 recommendations are:

- **Mihawk:** Top overall choice, especially with the rest spell build.
- **Robin:** One of the two decks slightly above the rest, highly strong and resilient.
- **Enel:** Very strong, particularly with multiple Enels, multiple Pinks, Laws, Frankies, and a balanced Gamma Knife / Gum-Gum Lightning package.
- **Red-Green Luffy Ace:** Strong, fast, and capable of exploiting the field, especially in quick-match structures.
- **Rocks:** Extremely strong into most decks, but too risky if Mihawk remains highly represented.
- **Sabo:** Less attractive because the rest spell and Yasopp give Mihawk much better early control.
- **Nami:** Not recommended; too many top decks punish it.
- **Boa:** Playable but too polarized, especially because Robin is a difficult matchup.

Impact’s event-level recommendation is to pick Mihawk, Robin, Enel, or Luffy Ace based on comfort and matchup knowledge. Do not abandon a practiced deck chasing a marginal theoretical edge. A player who understands their deck, curve, search targets, counter plan, and matchup strategy will gain more equity than a player who copies an untested list the night before.
