LawSopp vs HawkRona

Le due versioni del mazzo messe a confronto argomento per argomento — Law + Yasopp (Impact) contro Mihawk 6c + Perona 5c (samsansOP). Le 50 righe del documento sono raggruppate per tema: per ognuna la posizione di Sam, quella di Impact e cosa significa in pratica.

🆕 Aggiornamento 17/09

Il documento confronta le liste di fine agosto. Nel Q&A del 17/09 Impact indica tre build: la tempo/Perona (la più vicina a HawkRona, 1ª al regionale, «best in the mirror»), la rest-spell build con 4 «Faces of Trash» e 4 Coffin Boat (2ª al regionale, la sua scelta per il weekend) e l'ibrida 3 Law / 2 Hawk. La LawSopp di questa pagina è quindi la base da cui è partita la sua build attuale, non la build attuale. Le tre build nel Q&A →

🧭 Identità e filosofia

Primary deck identity

Sam · HawkRona

Aggressive midrange tuned for consistency, clean resource conversion, and mirror performance

Impact · LawSopp

Aggressive midrange tuned for board development, tempo, and rapid Shanks finishing

What it means in practice

Both are proactive decks. Neither teacher believes OP17 Mihawk should primarily play as a slow control deck.

Central philosophy

Sam · HawkRona

Reduce failed hands and reach the Shanks pivot with the right cards, board state, life total, and counter resources

Impact · LawSopp

Build a board quickly, use extra DON to attack efficiently, and force the opponent into Shanks kill range

What it means in practice

Sam favors a higher floor; Impact emphasizes a higher tempo ceiling.

Main source of advantage

Sam · HawkRona

Card quality, clean sequencing, filtering, freeze timing, and mirror disruption

Impact · LawSopp

Card volume, repeated searches, double development, resilient Yasopp pressure, and free-DON attacks

What it means in practice

Sam improves quality of resources; Impact increases quantity and tempo of resource conversion.

Main failure mode

Sam · HawkRona

Potentially gives up some direct board-control edge, especially into Sabo, by not fully exploiting Yasopp

Impact · LawSopp

Can draw the wrong mix of setup cards/bricks, lose Law targets, or face awkward Samurai/Law sequencing

What it means in practice

Pick Sam's list if you hate inconsistency; pick Impact's list if you hate losing the board early.

Skill test

Sam · HawkRona

Life mapping, counter thresholds, identifying when to skip lower-value development, and navigating the mirror bomb race

Impact · LawSopp

Sequencing searches before Law, Law target management, board protection, identifying damage breakpoints, and planning multistep lethal turns

What it means in practice

Both are difficult decks, but their difficult decisions occur at different points in the game.

Best player profile

Sam · HawkRona

Player who wants consistency, anticipates many Mihawk mirrors, and prefers a disciplined, calculated bomb-turn plan

Impact · LawSopp

Player who enjoys active board combat, technical sequencing, repeated Perona loops, and applying pressure every turn

What it means in practice

The "better" list may be less about raw power than about your local field and decision-making strengths.

Best metagame profile

Sam · HawkRona

Mihawk-heavy, open, uncertain, or interaction-heavy field where clean execution is paramount

Impact · LawSopp

Sabo-heavy, small-body-heavy, board-centric field where Yasopp can repeatedly produce tempo

What it means in practice

Sam's build hedges against expected mirrors; Impact's build attacks established boards.

Most transferable lesson

Sam · HawkRona

Do not confuse drawing more cards with improving hand quality; life/counter math determines whether Shanks actually wins

Impact · LawSopp

Do not play Mihawk passively; build the board, convert free DON into 7K pressure, and map the opponent into Shanks range

What it means in practice

Combining these lessons produces the strongest overall Mihawk fundamentals.

🃏 Il pacchetto da 5 e 6 costi

Preferred 5/6-cost package

Sam · HawkRona

6-cost Mihawk and Perona

Impact · LawSopp

Trafalgar Law and Yasopp, with Perona searcher as a reusable engine

What it means in practice

This is the main structural difference between the lists.

Why play 6c Mihawk?

Sam · HawkRona

It works independently, filters a card, can replace bricks, and produces a more stable development turn

Impact · LawSopp

Not the preferred centerpiece; Impact favors Law's ability to double-develop and recycle utility bodies

What it means in practice

Sam sees filtering as a major form of card advantage in a deck with several non-counter, high-cost cards.

Why play Law?

Sam · HawkRona

Law can be strong but is viewed as dependent on a good opener and surviving small bodies

Impact · LawSopp

Law is a core engine: pick up Perona/searchers, develop another major body, and keep chaining value

What it means in practice

The disagreement is whether Law's setup cost is a meaningful liability or an intended, manageable part of the deck's engine.

Why play Yasopp?

Sam · HawkRona

Strong card, especially against Sabo, but not enough payoff by itself to justify Law over Mihawk in a mirror-focused field

Impact · LawSopp

One of the deck's most important cards: early board control, resilient attacker, Law target, and Shanks support

What it means in practice

If you value early board interaction highly, Impact's evaluation of Yasopp becomes much more compelling.

Yasopp's self-restand

Sam · HawkRona

Helpful, but not viewed as decisive in the mirror

Impact · LawSopp

Central to the build: Yasopp can be developed rested through Law yet restand, making the Law turn less vulnerable

What it means in practice

Impact's LawSopp construction specifically solves the "Law leaves a body sideways" problem by making Yasopp the preferred deployment.

Perona's role (1c/5c)

Sam · HawkRona

Freeze, disruption, and a way to make meaningful turns when Shanks is unavailable or delayed

Impact · LawSopp

Extremely important searcher that should often be bounced by Law to find continuation pieces

What it means in practice

Both highly value Perona. Sam emphasizes her independent freeze/filter utility; Impact emphasizes repeated access through Law.

Perona + Law interaction

Sam · HawkRona

Recognized as good card flow, but Sam believes it does not outweigh Mihawk's independent filtering and board quality

Impact · LawSopp

A signature line: bounce Perona with Law and play a 5c, replay her, and look for Shanks, Luna, events, or follow-up plays

What it means in practice

LawSopp gains ceiling and deeper digging when it can repeatedly convert Perona.

⚙️ Motore, consistenza e difesa

Brick management

Sam · HawkRona

6c Mihawk is preferred because it can actively filter a brick out of hand

Impact · LawSopp

Use Samurai, Coffin Boat, Kinemon, leader-generated DON, and Law/Perona loops to avoid getting clogged

What it means in practice

Both acknowledge green Mihawk has bricks. Sam relies more on built-in filtering; Impact relies more on active card cycling and sequencing.

View of raw card draw

Sam · HawkRona

Drawing an extra card is not always better if the original brick remains in your hand

Impact · LawSopp

High draw density lets the deck find otherwise unsearchable pieces and maintain follow-up

What it means in practice

Sam differentiates "more cards" from "better cards"; Impact values the volume of looks generated by the deck's engines.

Opener dependence

Sam · HawkRona

Low-to-moderate: Mihawk still functions if the opponent clears early characters

Impact · LawSopp

Higher: Law is best when there is a worthwhile body to pick up and convert

What it means in practice

Sam's version is more resilient when early searchers die or when the opening hand is uneven.

Small-body vulnerability

Sam · HawkRona

Less reliant on small bodies remaining alive before the 5/6-cost turn

Impact · LawSopp

Needs early bodies to maximize Law, although the deck can develop many of them and convert them into value

What it means in practice

Against decks that efficiently remove 1-costs, Sam's structural argument gets stronger.

Samurai interaction

Sam · HawkRona

Samurai remains an important source of advantage; Sam dislikes that Law can make Samurai lines awkward

Impact · LawSopp

Samurai remains central, but Impact's guide emphasizes using excess DON to convert Samurai and avoid bricking

What it means in practice

Sam sees potential anti-synergy: Law wants a body to bounce, while Samurai can expose/remove the bodies Law needs.

Coffin Boat

Sam · HawkRona

Sam is lower on high event/Boat density than older builds because newer bomb turns frequently consume DON and some events matter less

Impact · LawSopp

Impact explicitly includes and uses Coffin Boat as part of the deck's card-flow and brick-management infrastructure

What it means in practice

This is a secondary but meaningful deck-construction difference. (Nota della guida: sul motivo di Sam vedi la ⚠️ Coffin Boat in Deck → Scelte di lista — nel video il ragionamento è diverso.)

2K counter philosophy

Sam · HawkRona

Strongly values ordinary 2K counter density because many mirror attacks are one-card hits

Impact · LawSopp

Emphasizes managing the deck's brick count and using counter events/resources without becoming clogged

What it means in practice

Sam's version leans toward reliable middle-game defense; Impact focuses more on actively converting awkward cards.

Defensive events

Sam · HawkRona

Be highly intentional about leaving DON up, especially because full Shanks/Luna turns consume most available DON

Impact · LawSopp

Generally spend around one DON per turn for events when appropriate, but calculate actual incoming swings and weave events out before they clog

What it means in practice

Both agree event management is a real skill test in the deck.

🗺️ Piano di gioco turno per turno

Early-game objective

Sam · HawkRona

Establish draw/search, preserve resources, and prepare the correct curve into the mirror/Shanks pivot

Impact · LawSopp

Draw cards, search for curve pieces, take early life when appropriate, and set up future development

What it means in practice

Both prioritize card access early; Impact is more explicit about taking life to acquire combo pieces.

Midgame objective

Sam · HawkRona

Develop enough pressure while preserving the correct hand, counter thresholds, and board state for Shanks

Impact · LawSopp

Establish 6K attackers, convert them into 7K swings with free DON, and pressure life every turn

What it means in practice

Sam is more cautious about resource structure; Impact is more explicit about relentless tempo conversion.

Use of extra DON

Sam · HawkRona

Spend it where it advances life mapping or allows important defensive/event management; avoid wasting it on low-value development

Impact · LawSopp

Use it aggressively to push damage, play searchers/Samurai, cycle bricks, and maintain board pressure

What it means in practice

Both recognize free DON as Mihawk's defining resource advantage. Impact pushes harder toward immediate damage.

Attacking philosophy

Sam · HawkRona

In the mirror, attack sizing and life preservation must be designed around the Shanks race

Impact · LawSopp

Attack aggressively, especially with 7K lines, to force the opponent into Shanks kill range

What it means in practice

Sam's attacks are more mirror-clock-focused; Impact's attacks are more universally pressure-focused.

Preferred board state

Sam · HawkRona

Stable, active bodies and a hand that can execute a Shanks turn without collapsing to counter pressure

Impact · LawSopp

Wide board of sticky 6K bodies that can repeatedly swing for 7K and force life/cards

What it means in practice

Sam prefers robustness; Impact wants density of attackers.

Board protection

Sam · HawkRona

Important, but Sam's analysis values whether the board actually survives the opponent's Shanks/Luna pivot

Impact · LawSopp

A major priority: midgame 6K bodies are how the deck converts wins, and Billionfold should often protect them

What it means in practice

Impact places particular weight on preserving 6K attackers because they become the bridge to Shanks lethal.

Role of freezes

Sam · HawkRona

Freeze effects are central to interrupting an opposing Shanks turn or reducing a board's conversion

Impact · LawSopp

Freezes are for tempo, not prolonged control; use them to buy the turn needed to kill

What it means in practice

This is mostly agreement. Neither wants to freeze merely to extend a game indefinitely.

View of long control games

Sam · HawkRona

Avoid them when possible; the build is designed to win the critical bomb turns efficiently

Impact · LawSopp

Explicitly rejects OP14-style long Law & Bepo control loops as the deck's main plan

What it means in practice

Strong agreement: OP17 Mihawk should finish, not endlessly stall.

🗡️ Shanks e i boss

Shanks evaluation

Sam · HawkRona

The defining bomb of the mirror: the 12K rush swing changes life thresholds and forces careful countering

Impact · LawSopp

"Does everything": finisher, removal, setup for removal, setup for Luna, and a fast closing mechanism

What it means in practice

Both see Shanks as the reason OP17 Mihawk's endgame is dramatically stronger than earlier versions.

Shanks timing

Sam · HawkRona

Do not delay the relevant Shanks turn in the mirror; if you wait, the opponent can resolve theirs first

Impact · LawSopp

Deploy Shanks in the late game and often aim to finish by the second 10-DON turn

What it means in practice

Both want timely Shanks; Sam stresses race math, while Impact stresses converting the midgame into a fast close.

Shanks + Luna

Sam · HawkRona

A decisive mirror sequence because Shanks can make the opponent's board vulnerable to Luna/freeze effects

Impact · LawSopp

A primary tempo tool: freeze the opposing board while Shanks pushes damage and prepares lethal

What it means in practice

Both see this pairing as powerful; Sam discusses it more as mirror interaction, Impact more as a general finishing pattern.

Shanks + Vander Decken

Sam · HawkRona

Key answer to high-cost bombs and an important reason Kaido has become less attractive

Impact · LawSopp

A major matchup tool for dealing with 7–8+ DON threats while maintaining your own pressure

What it means in practice

Both treat Decken as a premium answer to large characters, not merely a generic removal card.

Law & Bepo

Sam · HawkRona

A compact "second bomb" that can replace a complicated multi-card defensive sequence and buy an extra turn

Impact · LawSopp

Usually a one-of extension tool; useful when one extra turn is needed but no longer the deck's main identity

What it means in practice

Sam gives Law & Bepo more strategic weight in the mirror; Impact treats it as a powerful but secondary extension card.

View of 7c/9c Shanks lines

Sam · HawkRona

Less attractive because Vander Decken interacts favorably and the newer freeze environment reduces their value

Impact · LawSopp

Focus remains on 10c Shanks as the major endgame bomb

What it means in practice

Sam is more explicit that Decken has narrowed the usefulness of prior Shanks options.

🪞 Il mirror

Mirror priority

Sam · HawkRona

Highest deck-building priority; Sam expects Mihawk to be common enough that mirror equity should shape flex slots

Impact · LawSopp

Important matchup, but Impact's guide focuses on executing the core pressure curve and damage map

What it means in practice

Sam is more willing to sacrifice some non-mirror edge for mirror tools and consistency.

Mirror going second

Sam · HawkRona

Very desirable because the second player can establish the more favorable double-development/Shanks sequence

Impact · LawSopp

Also says to go second; follow the normal curve and use early draw to secure counter and bomb access

What it means in practice

Strong agreement: going second is preferred in the mirror.

Mirror life management

Sam · HawkRona

You often need to preserve at least two life before the opponent's Shanks turn because dropping to 0 is extremely dangerous

Impact · LawSopp

Counter smaller swings early because Shanks attacks can become too large to defend efficiently

What it means in practice

Sam gives more exact conceptual weight to life thresholds; Impact gives practical attack-size guidance.

Mirror attack thresholds

Sam · HawkRona

Focus is on putting the opponent to 0 first while retaining enough counter to survive retaliation

Impact · LawSopp

Recommends 7K frequently, 8K when no DON is up, 9K when 8 rested cards are unavailable, and 10K to attack through Billionfold

What it means in practice

Impact gives concrete breakpoints for the mirror.

Mirror tech: Stun Bonney

Sam · HawkRona

Major innovation in Sam's approach; can deny the opposing Shanks turn and reverse who gets the first bomb pivot

Impact · LawSopp

Not a central element of Impact's published standard list or matchup plan

What it means in practice

This is a substantial practical distinction. Sam sees Bonney as a way to fix going-first mirror weakness.

⚔️ Gli altri matchup

Sabo matchup

Sam · HawkRona

Acknowledges Yasopp meaningfully improves the matchup, but does not believe that alone outweighs HawkRona's mirror advantages

Impact · LawSopp

Identifies Sabo as one of the more difficult matchups and builds specifically to answer it through Yasopp, Oden, Decken, and careful countering

What it means in practice

Sabo is Impact's strongest practical argument for LawSopp.

Sabo plan

Sam · HawkRona

Attack proactively rather than attempting a full starvation strategy; selectively attack rested bodies when offered

Impact · LawSopp

Use Yasopp to clear small bodies, preserve Decken for Luffy/Loki, freeze high-impact units with Oden, and aim for a two-turn Shanks kill

What it means in practice

Sam is more skeptical of trying to completely control Sabo; Impact leans more into selective board control.

Sabo and Yasopp

Sam · HawkRona

Yasopp's key benefit is turning an opponent's unit sideways so you can contest it while retaining your own active body

Impact · LawSopp

Yasopp is the early answer that prevents Sabo's low-cost value bodies from snowballing

What it means in practice

Both agree Yasopp is good into Sabo; they differ on how much that should determine the full list.

Kaido matchup

Sam · HawkRona

Shanks + Vander Decken materially improves the matchup; Decken is a major reason Kaido is less appealing into Mihawk

Impact · LawSopp

Also emphasizes Shanks + Decken as a huge tempo exchange; Yasopp can help restand Shanks and attack safely

What it means in practice

Both see Decken as critical. Sam leans toward consistent access; Impact emphasizes on-board execution.

Kaido attack sequencing

Sam · HawkRona

Prioritize avoiding inefficient exchanges and maintaining the bomb-turn plan

Impact · LawSopp

Attack with 6K characters at 7K to play around leader reduction; use leader DON differently when setting up lethal

What it means in practice

Impact provides more specific tactical sequencing guidance.

Robin matchup

Sam · HawkRona

Stun Bonney and freeze-related options can incidentally improve yellow/mom board turns

Impact · LawSopp

Yasopp clears 4K units; pressure early so Robin cannot freely establish two Big Mom turns; use Shanks + Decken/Luna to remove key bodies

What it means in practice

Impact's guide gives a detailed Robin plan.

Rocks matchup

Sam · HawkRona

Mihawk is naturally strong; Law & Bepo can be an alternate method of invalidating their board

Impact · LawSopp

Use Luna heavily, account for Captain John stall, pressure with appropriate attack sizes, and respect Shiki

What it means in practice

Both are optimistic about the matchup.

Enel matchup

Sam · HawkRona

Preserve appropriate life and prepare Shanks turns; freezes can limit their board conversion

Impact · LawSopp

Use low-cost bodies to clear 1-costs, push life with leader, reserve Oden for large Enels, and avoid losing more than one life per turn

What it means in practice

Impact's guide covers defense and combo timing against Enel. (Nota della guida: Sam non tratta Enel nel video — la colonna Sam qui è un'estrapolazione dell'autore del documento.)

🎯 Fast Decision Table

Start with Sam's HawkRona

If this sounds like you…

  • "My local scene has a lot of Mihawk, and I want fewer hands where my key 5-cost is dead."
  • "I want my 5-DON play to be more self-contained and improve my hand quality."
  • "I regularly lose mirrors because I reach Shanks turns with the wrong life total or not enough counter."
  • "I want to try stun Bonney as a meaningful going-first mirror tool."
Start with Impact's LawSopp

If this sounds like you…

  • "My local scene has a lot of Sabo or decks that snowball through cheap value bodies."
  • "I want the most proactive board-control version of Mihawk."
  • "I am comfortable sequencing searchers, Samurai, Law targets, Perona bounces, and multi-card turns."
  • "I want Yasopp as both a midgame attacker and a way to protect/restand Shanks."
  • "I want to pressure life every turn and force opponents into a two-turn Shanks clock."
"I am unsure which to play and expect an unknown field." → Sam's HawkRona for reliability; then apply Impact's aggressive damage-mapping fundamentals.
