In 30 secondi

Mihawk verde è un midrange aggressivo con finisher rush: usa i 3 DON!! gratis del leader (più 1 dello stage) per sviluppare corpi da 6000 e spingere danno ogni turno, pesca tantissimo con searcher / Samurai / Kin'emon, e chiude con 10 Shanks (12000 rush che riposa tutto il board avversario) + Luna che congela quel board + Vander Decken che rimuove il corpo che conta. Law & Bepo resta a 1 copia come seconda bomba. Il mirror è il matchup più importante e si vince a chi arriva prima a Shanks + Luna: per questo vai secondo nel mirror e non scendere a 0 vite con leggerezza.
Entrambe le fonti concordano
  • Il deck vuole chiudere in fretta: non è più il Mihawk OP14 che allungava con Law/Bepo.
  • Shanks + Luna (+ Decken) è la fine partita che definisce OP17.
  • 1 sola Law & Bepo: seconda bomba, non piano principale.
  • Rad Beam / Billions vale più degli altri 3k perché difende i corpi a metà partita.
  • Mirror: vai secondo; contra i colpi piccoli perché Shanks colpisce troppo forte.
  • Non tenere in mano gli eventi: i DON!! finiscono tutti nel turno Shanks + Luna.
Dove le due versioni divergono

Cos'è il deck (e cosa non è)

Impact · Slide 3 — What The Deck Is
  • Midrange Deck
  • Rush Finisher
  • Builds Board by developing sticky characters quickly.
  • Finds follow-up plays consistently due to the immense amount of card draw.
  • Aggressive leaning.
  • Really fundamentals heavy deck. Requires good understanding of how to play midrange decks well. Requires good understanding of how to play One Piece well.
Impact · Slide 4 — What The Deck Isn't
  • Not a control deck.
  • Though it is able to control the board and win resource battles if it has to, your primary win condition is dealing damage, not grinding people out of the game.
  • The deck uses freezes to push tempo, not extend the game.
  • This isn't OP14 style Mihawk where Law and Bepo was used to extend games multiple turns and use boss monsters like Mihawk and Shanks to control the opponent. It is meant to end games quickly.
Impact · Slide 5 — Deck's Strengths
  • Very consistent. Key pieces are searchable with Otama and Perona.
  • Lots of card draw means you will be able to filter towards unsearchable pieces (Vander Decken, Yasopp, Coffin Boat)
  • Deck cheats an unbelievable amount of DON every turn. 4 extra DON between leader and stage.
  • Deck is strong at racing because of Shanks and Luna.
  • Deck is strong at ending games quickly because the excess DON from leader skill lets you develop attackers while attacking for more damage.
Impact · Slide 6 — Decks Weaknesses
  • Deck can be weak to early aggression or explosive starts.
  • The deck has a higher than normal brick count (It is a GREEN deck so it is to be expected) so it requires a little bit more micro managing than most decks.
  • Deck can be weak to large characters that are outside of Luna range if you don't have Vander Decken early.
  • Other than that - no real weaknesses. This is an incredibly overtuned deck.

Identità OP17: cosa è cambiato da OP16.5

Sam · Session Overview

Sam OP17 Mihawk session focuses on how the deck has changed from OP16.5 and why the new Shanks, Vander Decken, Luna, and stun package has reshaped both deckbuilding and matchup strategy. Her broad conclusion is:

  • Mihawk is likely the strongest overall deck in the current OP17 environment.
  • It is not necessarily Tier 0, and Kaido, Sabo, Robin, and other decks remain valid tournament choices.
  • Mihawk's mirror is the matchup that matters most for serious tournament preparation.
  • The deck's main advantage is no longer only Law/Bepo endgames. It now has a Shanks plus Vander Decken plus Luna endgame that can invalidate entire boards.
  • Deck construction should prioritize strong mirror performance while retaining enough tools to beat Sabo, Rocks, Kaido, yellow Big Mom decks, and other important field decks.

Sam · The OP17 Mihawk Game Plan

OP17 Mihawk is still fundamentally built around the same strong Wano core: Searchers · Samurai effects · Perona · Oden · Mihawk · Law/Bepo · Rad Beam · Efficient board development · Strong event-based defense.

The deck still wants to:

  1. Develop a 5-cost body on its first relevant curve turn.
  2. Build efficient 6-cost and 5-cost combinations.
  3. Use Perona and Oden to prevent the opponent's board from converting.
  4. Generate card advantage through Samurai, searchers, Mihawk filtering, and Oden.
  5. Transition into a late-game bomb turn.
  6. Use high leader power, Luna, Shanks, Vander Decken, or Law/Bepo to finish.

What has changed is the endgame. In OP16.5, Mihawk mirrors often became Law/Bepo wars. Stun cards were less important because the opponent's board frequently could not convert anyway. In OP17, Shanks changes that. When Mihawk plays Shanks:

  • Opponent's board can be rested.
  • Luna can freeze the board.
  • Vander Decken can remove a key large body.
  • Mihawk can attack with a 12k threat on the same turn.
  • The opponent is often pushed to zero life while their board is disabled.

This means stuns now have much more value because Shanks creates the board state that makes Luna devastating.

Il turno Shanks (Sam)
++
  1. Establish enough board earlier in the game.
  2. Play Shanks.
  3. Rest or force opposing bodies sideways.
  4. Use Vander Decken to remove the most important target.
  5. Use Luna to freeze the remaining board.
  6. Attack with a 12k body and other existing attackers.
  7. Put the opponent at zero or nearly zero.
  8. Force the opponent to respond immediately without a functional board.

Shanks is especially strong in the mirror because it removes the old assumption that medium bodies automatically retain value into Law/Bepo turns.

Sam · Wano Core

Sam considers the Wano engine largely unchanged and non-negotiable. The core gives Mihawk: reliable early development · card selection · Samurai conversion · access to multiple 6-cost plays · Perona and Oden lines · filtered access to major threats · a consistent way to generate value over a long game.

She is very high on playing four Samurai effects. The deck wants to see several in a game, especially in the mirror where extra draws, extra filtering, and card economy are major sources of edge. If Samurai becomes unusable late, it can still be pitched through Mihawk filtering. That makes it much less punishing to play four copies.

Confronto shell: Mihawk + Perona vs Law + Yasopp

+oppure+
Sam · Mihawk vs Law Shells

A major list decision is whether to play the Mihawk plus Perona shell or the Law plus Yasopp shell.

Sam · Mihawk plus Perona (la sua scelta)

Sam prefers Mihawk plus Perona. Advantages:

  • Mihawk is larger.
  • Mihawk's developed body remains standing.
  • A standing body is much harder for the opponent to contest.
  • Mihawk is better in the mirror.
  • Mihawk is better into larger-bomb decks such as Kaido.
  • Mihawk filters bricks out of hand.
  • The filtering effect can be equivalent to, or better than, Law's extra card advantage.
  • Perona remains strong into Sabo.
  • Perona protects against KO patterns.
  • Perona can stop two relevant 6k attacks in Sabo board states.
Sam · Law plus Yasopp (l'alternativa)

Law plus Yasopp is a valid alternative shell. Advantages:

  • Yasopp is strong on exactly five DON!!.
  • Yasopp attacks, rests, then stands again.
  • Law can place Yasopp without leaving the new body sideways.
  • Yasopp can attack opposing small bodies and help contest board.
  • The shell improves some Sabo scenarios.

Concerns:

  • Law is worse than Mihawk in the mirror.
  • Law relies more on a good opener and surviving one-drops.
  • Law and Samurai have anti-synergy because Samurai can expose the one-drops Law needs.
  • Law does not filter bricks.
  • Yasopp is not as valuable in all matchups as it appears.
  • Yasopp is weak into Ace Luffy because Ace's bodies are too large for Yasopp to contest.
  • Perona has meaningful Sabo interaction that Yasopp does not replicate.

Sam's bottom line is that Law is mainly played to gain Yasopp access. She does not believe Yasopp is good enough to justify replacing the stronger Mihawk plus Perona package in her current build.

Vander Decken

+
Sam · Vander Decken

Vander Decken has become close to standard in OP17 Mihawk. Its main purpose is to improve the Sabo matchup. Why it matters against Sabo:

  • Sabo creates a board full of Giant-enabled bodies.
  • Its big units can become difficult to contest conventionally.
  • Vander Decken lets Mihawk remove or answer an important body.
  • It gives Mihawk a cleaner way to interact with the Sabo board before it becomes overwhelming.

Vander Decken is also incidentally good in the mirror: if the opponent plays Shanks, Mihawk can interact with the Shanks; if the opponent plays a major body, Decken provides a practical answer; it supports Shanks plus Luna lines by removing the character that matters most. Sam does not describe Decken as the only mirror plan, but it is a major tool in the most common Shanks endgame.

Shanks e Luna

Sam · Shanks

Shanks is the defining OP17 Mihawk bomb. Its role is not merely to remove a body. Its real value is that it creates a board state where Luna becomes a mass tempo swing.

Sam · Luna

Luna is now much more important than it was in OP16.5. In the old Law/Bepo-focused Mihawk endgame, the opponent's board often already struggled to attack into a large leader. Stuns were less necessary. In OP17:

  • Shanks rests the opposing board.
  • Luna freezes the entire group.
  • Mihawk gets a tempo turn without taking meaningful return pressure.
  • The opponent often cannot make up the lost turn because Mihawk's board is already attacking for large numbers.

Luna is not merely defensive. It is a card that converts Shanks from a strong threat into a game-ending tempo package.

Stun Bonney

Sam · Stun Bonney

Sam is testing Stun Bonney as mirror tech. The key use is simple: Stun Bonney can take the opponent off its Shanks turn.

In the mirror, the player going second often has the natural advantage of playing Shanks first and then using Luna to punish the opponent's board. Stun Bonney gives the going-first player a way to disrupt that timing. Why Bonney is playable even in a Mihawk build:

  • Law into Bonney creates a body that often enters sideways anyway.
  • That body can be removed by Luna, combat, or other interaction.
  • The difference between Law plus Bonney and standalone Bonney on the relevant turn may be smaller than it appears.

Andando primo nel mirror: il problema strutturale (Sam)

Sam · The New Mirror Problem

The player going first has a difficult structural issue. If both players develop optimally:

  • The going-second player reaches Shanks plus Luna first.
  • The going-first player may have a wide board.
  • Shanks rests the board.
  • Luna freezes it.
  • The going-second player attacks with a 12k Shanks plus additional bodies.
  • The going-first player can be pushed to zero.
  • Mihawk at zero has limited reliable ways to play another turn.

This is why leader life management has changed in OP17.

Sam · Stun Bonney as the Going-First Plan

Stun Bonney is the main experimental answer for the player going first. If Bonney prevents the opponent from playing Shanks on curve:

  • Their planned Shanks plus Luna line becomes unavailable.
  • Their 9-DON!! turn becomes awkward.
  • They may only be able to Perona a body or attack a single rested character.
  • They do not establish a major bomb.
  • The going-first player becomes the first player who can Shanks plus Luna.

The opponent may still send several attacks, so the Mihawk player must be willing to counter enough damage to survive. However, if the opponent cannot play Shanks, the Mihawk player can often safely go to one life because the opponent's follow-up is much weaker.

Sam · Going-First Pressure Plan

If Stun Bonney is unavailable, Sam recommends using the odd-DON!! turns to push life efficiently. On 9 DON!!:

  • Do not automatically play another 6-cost body.
  • The opponent will often Shanks plus Luna the board anyway.
  • The extra body may not matter.
  • Use the available DON!! to make larger attacks.
  • Play Perona to reduce the incoming attack count.
  • Push the opponent toward one or zero life before their bomb turn.

The logic is simple: if both players reach one life, the player who zeros the opponent first wins the initiative. The opponent then has to kill immediately. If the opponent cannot kill through your counter and leader power, you win the following turn.

Sam · Why 6-Cost on 9 Is Worse

The 6-cost body on 9 DON!! is less valuable than it was in OP16.5 because the opponent's Shanks plus Luna turn can invalidate the entire board. The player going first should often choose: Perona plus larger attacks · Stun Bonney plus development · more aggressive face pressure · a defensive event posture — instead of 6-cost development that is immediately frozen with the rest of the board.

On 10 DON!!, Mihawk should almost always play its bomb. Why:

  • Delaying lets the opponent play their bomb first.
  • The game becomes a race to zero the opponent before they zero you.
  • Shanks creates immediate 12k pressure.
  • Law/Bepo can compress multiple interaction effects into one card.
  • Waiting generally creates no real advantage.

The rare exception is a Stun Bonney plus another development line that prevents the opponent's entire bomb turn. In most games, however, Mihawk should simply play the 10-cost bomb.

Sam · Life Management (mirror)

Mihawk cannot casually go to zero against Shanks Mihawk. Why zero is dangerous:

  • Mihawk has limited blocker access.
  • Law can be answered by Oden, Shanks, DMG, or other interaction.
  • The opponent can create a 12k swing on its bomb turn.
  • The opponent can freeze the board and remove the ability to defend through combat.
  • Once at zero, Mihawk may have no reliable way to survive a second large push.

Sam's guideline: try to be at least two life at the beginning of the opponent's 10-DON!! turn. Sometimes two is sufficient if Rad Beam and counter math cover their next turn. In many games, this requires countering more of the one-card hits in the middle game than OP16.5 Mihawk did.

Sam · Rad Beam and Event Timing

The mirror has more turns where events can become stuck. If you use Shanks, Vander Decken, Luna you often spend all available DON!!. That means a Rad Beam held too long can become unusable. The practical rule:

  • Use Rad Beam on the turn before the all-in Shanks plus Luna turn if it saves a meaningful body or preserves enough life.
  • Do not hold every Rad Beam just because it looks valuable.
  • Plan one turn ahead: if the next turn uses all DON!!, convert your event now or it may remain dead in hand.

Principi generali (Sam)

Sam · Build for the Mirror

Sam's deckbuilding priority is mirror equity. Why:

  • Mihawk is expected to be extremely popular.
  • Mihawk is likely overrepresented among strong players.
  • Mihawk is likely overrepresented in top cut.
  • Losing the mirror repeatedly is unacceptable for a tournament deck.

Stun Bonney, Shanks, Luna, Vander Decken, and Law/Bepo all contribute directly or indirectly to mirror success.

Sam · Know Which Attack Matters

Before using DON!! for an attack, ask:

  • Does this swing force a real card?
  • Does this attack put the opponent at one or zero?
  • Does this remove a character that enables their board?
  • Does it create a two-turn lethal?
  • Is the DON!! better spent on Samurai, a searcher, Rad Beam, or a development?

Sam · Matchup Identity

The mirror is the most important matchup in OP17 Mihawk. Sam expects Mihawk to be: the most popular deck · the deck most likely to be present in top cut · the deck every serious Mihawk list must prepare to face repeatedly.

The mirror is no longer just a Law/Bepo war. It is now a race to:

  • Preserve life correctly.
  • Play Shanks first.
  • Use Luna effectively.

Sam · Matchup Identity

Sam sees Sabo as Mihawk's worst major matchup. The matchup is more difficult when Mihawk goes first because:

  • Mihawk reaches its pivot bomb turn one turn later.
  • Sabo can build a wider board before Mihawk gets Shanks or Law/Bepo active.

Sam · Yasopp Versus Perona

The Law/Yasopp build has more direct value into Sabo because Yasopp can rest a 2-cost body and attack it. However, Sam does not think this alone makes Law/Yasopp clearly superior. Perona is also strong:

  • It can stun two 6k bodies.
  • It protects against removal by replacing itself. ⚠️ nel video: "the Perona will float into something if it dies, so you can just let it die and summon an Oden" — è l'On K.O. che gioca un altro 5 costi
  • It can prevent two meaningful attacks when Sabo plays two 2-cost bodies plus Saul or Docking Station.
  • It can reduce Sabo's ability to attack in a coordinated wave.

The current Mihawk plus Perona build gives up some Sabo equity compared with Yasopp, but Sam does not think the sacrifice is large enough to abandon the stronger mirror shell.

Sam · Attack Versus Starve

Without Yasopp, Sam does not recommend fully starving Sabo. Why not:

  • Sabo can continue developing.
  • Its cards search, replace themselves, or create board value.
  • A full starve lets Sabo construct its ideal board.
  • Mihawk loses opportunities to create two-turn lethal pressure.
  • If Mihawk gets knocked low while starving, it may have no winning route because Sabo still has a board.

Her default is to attack Sabo. The preferred pattern:

  1. Deal the first damage.
  2. If Sabo turns a body sideways, attack that body.
  3. If Sabo lets the body die, continue attacking face.
  4. If Sabo defends the body, keep attacking face and let them spend their hand.
  5. Transition into a race once Sabo is low.

This gives Mihawk multiple win conditions: Sabo runs out of hand protecting the board · Sabo takes too much life and dies to Shanks · Mihawk clears enough Giant bodies to invalidate the board · Law/Bepo turns the board off and enables a two-turn lethal.

Sam · When to Attack Sabo's Board

Mihawk should attack Sabo's board when: a character is already rested · the attack does not require too much extra DON!! · the body is important to Sabo's Giant engine · the body is a 6k attacker that will create future pressure · killing it prevents Sabo from converting its board.

Mihawk should not attach DON!! merely to attack into Saul unless: Sabo's hand is already very small · killing Saul turns off the entire board · the attack prevents immediate lethal · you are removing the final Giant body that makes their next turn functional.

Sam · Why Attacking Helps Against Gravity Blade

Attacking Sabo throughout the game makes Gravity Blade less punishing. If Mihawk starves Sabo and Sabo resolves Gravity Blade: Mihawk may be at two life · Sabo may still be at five life · Mihawk's board is reduced · Mihawk has a hard time closing the life gap. If Mihawk has already attacked Sabo: Sabo is lower on life · Sabo has already spent counter · Mihawk's surviving bodies still threaten life · a Gravity Blade turn does not automatically erase the win condition · Shanks can still create a lethal turn.

Sam · Law/Bepo in the Matchup

Law/Bepo is strong against Sabo because Sabo often cannot play around it. Sabo expects Shanks and generally wants to use its board to attack. If it turns bodies: Law/Bepo can stun a key Giant or Loki · Mihawk gains leader power · Sabo's smaller bodies become much less effective · Mihawk can push lethal over two turns while holding enough DON!! to survive.

The key is that Law/Bepo is poor in a full-starve game. If Sabo remains at five life, gaining one extra turn does not solve the matchup. Attacking first makes Law/Bepo a real finisher.

Sam · Matchup Identity / Main Plan

Sam believes Mihawk is favored into yellow Big Mom decks, including Robin-style shells. The reason is that Mihawk devalues the ways yellow decks want to convert board. Yellow decks want to: build wide boards · play Big Mom · heal · use trigger bodies · attack with several mid-sized characters · force the opponent to spend cards defending life. Mihawk answers this with: Perona freezes · Oden freezes · Shanks plus Vander Decken · Luna · Law/Bepo · high leader power · repeated double development.

Mihawk should: take early damage when safe · develop bodies · freeze relevant 4k and 5k attackers · prevent Big Mom board turns from converting · push the opponent low before they establish repeated healing · use Shanks to remove the major body · use Luna to freeze triggers and smaller attackers · force the yellow player to attempt lethal from a low-counter position.

Sam · Stun Bonney Role

Stun Bonney is particularly useful into yellow Big Mom decks. If Mihawk stuns the opponent on their first major Big Mom turn: they may have no productive 10-DON!! play · their whole curve can become delayed · Mihawk gets another turn to develop · the opponent's board is still too narrow to pressure effectively · Mihawk can often take damage safely and then execute a bomb turn. The main exception is Pudding, which may have alternative plays and more flexible development.

Sam · Robin-Specific Notes
  • Counter banish hits more often than against many other decks because Robin's available DON!! is constrained.
  • The banish attacks are frequently small enough that countering is efficient.
  • Taking the first damage is often fine.
  • If Stun Bonney is available, Mihawk can take damage more liberally because Robin cannot execute the first 10-DON!! turn.

  • If the opponent is low on hand, force the heal because discarding the extra card becomes a real cost.
  • Never allow a heal if you are currently presenting lethal.

Why Mihawk wins: Mihawk can repeatedly freeze yellow's small attackers · survive while preserving board · prevent the Big Mom board from converting · put yellow low enough that a Shanks 12k swing becomes impossible to defend · force yellow to spend too many attacks into Mihawk's board, leaving Mihawk healthy enough to survive a final push.
